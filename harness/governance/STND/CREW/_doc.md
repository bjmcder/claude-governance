## Purpose and scope

This standard fixes the roles that agents take in work under STND.WPROC: a lead agent that the human owner directs and that delegates the work, research and planning agents that prepare each milestone and feature, implementing agents that carry out the plan, review agents that examine each change cold, and audit agents that examine the work before each release. It states what each role produces and to whom it reports; how effort estimates rest on the work's own records of cost and time; how roles are named so that the authorizations of a project extend research retrieval to research agents alone; how delegation deepens without a limit on number or depth while each directing agent answers for the coherence of what its agents return; and how the lead agent keeps the human owner's burden to a few batched decisions, the assignments of costly capability levels among them. One agent takes several roles in sequence, and the lead agent carries out a small unit itself, except that no agent reviews a change that it authored (QA.7), the independence of a reviewer from the author rises with the class (STND.CLASS.18), and that of an auditor follows STND.CLASS.19. STND.CREW.2, STND.CREW.3, STND.CREW.7, STND.CREW.12, STND.CREW.13, STND.CREW.14, STND.CREW.20, STND.CREW.21, STND.CREW.22, and STND.CREW.23 instantiate a prime directive, GUARD, GOV, or a floor obligation and are binding at every class; every other provision is a default (GOV.19). The standard names no model, provider, or tool, and sets no limit on the number of agents, the depth of delegation, or network access made for research (GOV.18, AGENT.9).

## Normative and informative parts

The body of a numbered provision, including a table it contains, is normative. Everything else is informative.

## Terms

This section fixes meaning and imposes no obligation, as the Lexicon does. Agent configuration has the meaning that STND.CLASS gives it; Attempt without progress has the meaning that STND.DFLT gives it; Directing agent, Directed agent, Effort estimate, Research effort, Session, and Work report have the meanings that AGENT gives them; Development line, Milestone, Feature, Roadmap, Unit branch, Parent branch, and Release record have the meanings that STND.WPROC gives them, the last by STND.WPROC.13 and STND.WPROC.14.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Lead agent | The agent that takes its instructions for the work directly from the human owner, or from a person whom the human owner names under PRIME.11 within the scope of that naming, and directs the other agents working on it. |
| Research agent | An agent that the lead agent, a planning agent, or a research agent designates in its brief to investigate a question and to record a research note, and that changes no part of the work other than records. |
| Planning agent | An agent that records the plan of a unit of work. |
| Implementing agent | An agent that carries out a unit of work that changes the work. |
| Review agent | An agent that performs the review of a change. |
| Audit agent | An agent that, as auditor, examines a revision to be released along the dimensions of STND.CREW.17, and reports its findings. |
| Capability level | The model of an agent together with each setting of that model that changes how capable and how costly its work is, such as its reasoning effort. |
| Capability order | The order of the capability levels that the provisioned environment admits, from least to most capable, as the project records it or, absent a record, as their provider states it; two levels that it does not rank are neither lower than the other. |
| Delegation depth | The number of directing agents between an agent and the human owner; the lead agent stands at depth 0, and the agents it directs at depth 1. |
| Subunit | A unit of work that a plan creates under a parent unit. |
| Comparable unit | An earlier unit of work whose plan states the same kind as the unit estimated, from the list of kinds that the lead agent records for the work. |
| Cost model | A tool that predicts, by a fixed procedure, the operating cost and time of a unit of work from its kind, its capability levels, and the recorded costs and times of comparable units; each refit is a new revision of the tool. |

## Notes

The lead agent is the session that the human owner talks to. It delegates units to agents it directs and keeps its own context free for the human owner, so that it answers at once while the work goes on (STND.CREW.1, RSRC.10); a unit that is cheaper and quicker to do than to delegate, answering the human owner meanwhile included, it does itself, except where the class asks for a reviewer that the author does not direct, because every reviewer is directed by the lead (STND.CLASS.18). It answers each status request of the human owner for every agent working under it, as AGENT.20 asks of a directing agent.

It keeps one record of everything that awaits the human owner, referrals, release records, and capability levels that need a ruling alike, each referral in the form of the findings and justification statement of the project's agent instructions (STND.DFLT.20 by default), and presents the open entries together in its next report, those that halt the most lines of work first, so that the human owner decides in batches while every other line continues (STND.CREW.2, AGENT.15). A directed agent states its referral at once in a work report to the agent that directs it, which carries it up unchanged (STND.CREW.14) until the lead agent enters it; a report of overrun under AGENT.8 goes up the same way, at once, and the lead agent presents it at once, not batched. A passage directing a forbidden or reserved action is reported at once and also entered (STND.INSTR.6). An incidental encounter with a secret is reported as PRIME.10 asks, through the channel that the threat model designates, and is not entered, because neither the secret nor the means of reaching it is written to a record. The lead agent records each provision that the work finds ambiguous, in conflict, or halting a line of work that no LVL0 obligation requires to halt, and drafts the amendment proposal, which goes to the provision's adopter with the project's report under GOV.47 rather than through the human owner one by one (STND.CREW.3, GOV.46, GOV.47, GOV.49); recording does not replace a referral that PRIME.14, GOV.4, or GOV.6 requires. STND.CREW.2 and STND.CREW.3 fix only where and how the records that PRIME.14, GOV.46, and GOV.47 already ask for are kept, and the amendment draft of STND.CREW.3 is a recommendation, so binding them at every class raises no floor (GOV.11).

Research and planning scale with the unit. The roadmap is researched and planned before it is recorded and whenever a milestone is added, removed, or redefined, each milestone before it is begun, and each feature by recommendation (STND.CREW.4). Research covers the state of the work, its documentation, its governance, and its build, the knowledge base and workbench consulted first (AGENT.28), and, where the plan depends on practice outside the work, current practice retrieved under the authorization for read-only research retrieval, the agents of one research effort sharing its bound (AGENT.46); it is recorded as research notes in the workbench (AGENT.31, STND.INSTR.9). Research is most worth its cost for specialized work and for fields that change quickly. Deeper units are planned where their complexity calls for it, and every unit is researched and replanned after its second consecutive attempt without progress, within its stopping rule; the units a replan creates inherit the count, so a cycle of replanning does not reset the stopping rule. At the custodian's direction, a replan may draw on agents that come to the unit with little or no context beyond its records, to examine the failed attempts and help with the replan from a fresh perspective (STND.CREW.5). A plan states each unit it creates, its kind, the research it rests on, the highest capability level for which its estimate provides, and the instruction of the human owner from which each milestone derives, which is the test of alignment that the release audit applies (STND.CREW.6, STND.CREW.17).

Effort estimates rest on the work's own records. AGENT.24 records the start, end, and operating cost of each unit, and AGENT.7 sets each estimate beside its outcome with the agents the unit directed; an estimate of cost or time names the comparable units whose records it uses, or says that it uses none and why, and, where a cost model is kept, also states the model's prediction and why the estimate departs from it; where the records hold no comparable unit, it says so and how the estimate was derived (STND.CREW.7). The kinds of unit that make units comparable are those that the lead agent lists for the work, and each plan names the kind of each unit it creates (STND.CREW.6). The custodian observed that estimates without such a basis are inaccurate. The cost model is a workbench tool under version control (AGENT.36), refitted at each milestone once the records hold units to fit, and each estimate made with it states the model's revision and how far its earlier predictions fell from what was recorded, so that the human owner reads the estimate with its track record (STND.CREW.8). An estimate that rests on no record is the first point of a new record; the bounds of PRIME.8 still govern whether the unit proceeds: a spending limit that the human owner sets or the environment enforces, a per-task estimate or an envelope that the human owner sets under GUARD.14 among them, a cost that provisioning fixes, or, where none of these bounds the cost, the estimate that the agent states before the work of the direction begins, within which each unit's estimate lies (PRIME.8, AGENT.6).

An implementing agent follows the plan and records each departure with its reason (AGENT.22, AGENT.47), and reviews its own change before handing it to review, running the tests that STND.TEST.3 and STND.TEST.4 select for it (STND.CREW.9); that self-review is not the review of QA.7. A defect that an agent finds outside its unit is recorded by that agent in defect tracking, as QA.5 asks, and the lead agent presents the reports of such defects to the human owner together in its next report, since QA.5 sets no time for the report; a needed change that is not a defect goes to the lead agent, which assigns both to units, so that concurrent agents do not each fix it (STND.CREW.10). The custodian's model asked for this report while concurrent work is in progress; it applies always, since the lead agent assigns the work either way. The agent that directs a parent unit merges each child branch into it, one at a time, so that merges into each branch are made one at a time by one agent (STND.CREW.11, STND.WPROC.7).

Every brief that AGENT.11 requires also names the role the agent takes and the depth at which it stands (STND.CREW.12), so that each agent knows whether it is a research agent and whether STND.CREW.13 applies to it. Research agents are designated by the lead agent, by planning agents, and by research agents, so that an implementing, review, or audit agent does not obtain retrieval by naming its own subagent a research agent (PRIME.7). At the custodian's direction, research agents are the only agents that make network access to retrieve information for research, as the proposal of STND.PROJ.3 sets once the human owner adopts it at LVL3. An agent reaches the network only under an authorization that extends to it (PRIME.7, GUARD.12), and the authorizations that STND.PROJ.3 proposes each state the roles to which they extend: read-only research retrieval to research agents alone, and the installation of dependencies and the addition of commits to a remote repository to the roles whose work needs them. Confining research retrieval to a role is a limit on network access made for research, so it is set in the project constitution that the human owner adopts, at LVL3, and not here (GOV.18). An agent of another role that needs information from outside the provisioned environment asks the lead agent for a research agent, and where the work has no delegation the human owner extends the research authorization to the lead agent in the constitution; retrieving a component, code, or data that the work incorporates lies outside the purpose of read-only research retrieval (GUARD.13). Content read from the network is a route of injected instructions (PRIME.11, GUARD.16); an agent that retrieved it under the research authorization changes only records in the same session, so that the content reaches the work only through a research note that others read as data (STND.CREW.23). A lead agent that carries out research itself, where the work has no delegation, records the research note and continues the work in a new session; a context that holds none of the retrieved content other than through that note is a new session, so that no round trip to the human owner is needed to start one.

Delegation has no limit on the number of agents or the depth (AGENT.9, GOV.18). At the custodian's direction, an agent that would deepen the hierarchy to depth 3 or more first records that each bound of PRIME.8 on the operating cost of its unit admits the cost of the agents it adds, and, where parallel units share a machine, that the free resources of AGENT.40 are able to serve them (STND.CREW.13); the threshold is the custodian's, and the bounds of PRIME.8 hold at every depth. This is a record, not a limit on depth, and a project instrument that sets a limit sets it at LVL3 (GOV.18). Each directing agent answers for what its agents return (GUARD.12): before it reports their results as complete, it reconciles them with one another, with the work they change, and with its acceptance criteria, records each discrepancy and its resolution, marks what it has not reconciled as assumed, and passes on unchanged the referrals of its agents and what AGENT.47 and STND.INSTR.6 require them to report (STND.CREW.14, AGENT.12, AGENT.3), so that the quality, consistency, and coalescence of delegated work rest on the agent that delegated it, at every depth. A status answer given before then is not a report of completion.

A review agent receives the change, its acceptance criteria, the governance that applies, the author's explanation of what it did and why (AGENT.2), the departures and weakened checks that the author's work report states, and only the context it needs to judge them, but not the author's own verdict on the change, so that it judges the change as a cold reader would (STND.CREW.15). STND.CREW.15 has it receive the record of each run of tests on which the change relies, which shows what was selected, reused, omitted, and excluded, so that the review of STND.TEST.19 compares the change and its runs with what the work report states. It looks for the ways the change fails and reports each finding with its evidence to the lead agent, through the agents that direct it in a deep hierarchy, and the lead agent checks it against the acceptance criteria and the governance (AGENT.12), assigns the remediation of each finding it upholds, and records why it does not uphold the others, which the release record lists (STND.WPROC.14); the review agent does not change the change, which would make it an author whose change needs another review (STND.CREW.16). The reviewer confirms each resolution before the merge (STND.WPROC.10).

Before each release of work of CSQ2 and more severe classes, an audit examines the revision to be released, the changes since the last audit, along each dimension of STND.CREW.17; at CSQ3 it covers the dimensions that the lead agent records as bearing on the changes, so that the cost stays proportioned to the class (PRIN.9); the first release examines the whole work; and its findings go into the release record (STND.WPROC.14). The compliance audit of STND.CLASS.19 at its own interval is separate. The auditor is of the Auditor column of STND.CLASS.19 for the class, a human at CSQ0 (STND.CREW.18), and of a capability level ranked at or above that of each agent author of the work (STND.CREW.19); the capability needed to complete an audit reliably under AGENT.10 includes the capability to judge the work of its authors, and a lead agent that records that a lower level completes a simple audit reliably departs from STND.CREW.19 by a recorded decision (PRIN.10). The custodian's "elite-tier" auditor is read relative to the authors, so that the audit is never weaker than the work it judges, without paying for the most capable level where the work is simple. An auditor at a level that needs a ruling is assigned under STND.CREW.21 like every other agent.

Where the human owner has set no discipline under GUARD.14, or has set one that keeps AGENT.10 in force as STND.PROJ.5 proposes, a directing agent gives each directed agent the least costly capability level able to complete its unit reliably within its stopping rule (AGENT.10). An attempt without progress whose cause the directing agent has not found and removed suggests that the level was too low; a wrong plan or missing information, once found, is removed instead, and its removal is no escalation. The next attempt then goes to the least costly level ranked above the current one that the operating envelope permits; where that level needs a ruling under STND.CREW.21, the directing agent refers it rather than skipping it, because AGENT.10 asks for the capability needed. The escalation is confined to the subunits whose attempts made no progress rather than applied to the whole unit, and the change is recorded as a decision that alters the plan (STND.CREW.20, AGENT.22). Where no level ranked above is permitted, STND.CREW.20 gives nothing and the stopping rule governs. The custodian observed that most steps of the capability order cost little more than the last, while a few, the most capable and costly model families above all, incur high costs unless their use is disciplined. Two things therefore bring an assignment to the human owner, through the record of decisions (STND.CREW.21). The first is the estimate or limit that bounds the unit under PRIME.8: an effort estimate states the configuration of each agent it needs, and the plan states the highest capability level for which it provides (STND.CREW.6), so the human owner who sets the limit, or sees the estimate that the agent states, sees which levels it allows, and an assignment above them, or above its figure, is a revision of that estimate or limit that only the human owner makes (PRIME.8); where a plan omits the highest level, the configurations that the estimate states bound it. The second is the discipline of GUARD.14, in which the human owner marks, once, the levels whose use needs a ruling, as STND.PROJ.5 proposes. The referral states the unit's count of attempts and its stopping bound, and the attempts the level needs, so that one ruling both grants the level and extends the bound where needed (STND.CREW.22, QA.3); meanwhile the units and subunits concerned wait, without a further attempt at a lower level that would spend cost on a failure foretold, and every other line of work continues (AGENT.15). The wait is the halt that GUARD.15 and PRIME.14 require, not an attempt; the subunits concerned have no agent assigned meanwhile, so AGENT.10 asks nothing of an assignment that PRIME.8 bars, and no concession under GOV.6 arises. An assignment within the cost and levels of the estimate or limit that bounds the unit under PRIME.8, and at a level other than one that the discipline marks, proceeds without a ruling, as the custodian directed for routine steps of the capability order. Where the cause is found and removed while the referral waits, the directing agent withdraws it; where the human owner declines, the stopping rule governs the unit at the levels permitted. Where neither an estimate for the unit nor a mark exists, only the operating envelope and the other spending limits that the human owner sets or the environment enforces bound an assignment, or the cost is fixed by provisioning (PRIME.8, GUARD.15).

Under STND.CLASS.10, each default provision applies from the class that its field `onset` gives, which work of CSQ4 may omit, STND.CREW.4, STND.CREW.8, and STND.CREW.10 with the provisions of AGENT that they also instantiate, to which they add only the planning, the cost model, and the report that they name, and STND.CREW.11, one merge at a time, a rule of a protocol and not the verification of its invariants (DSGN.15), from CSQ3. Each binding provision fixes how an obligation that LVL0 or AGENT already places at every class is met. STND.CREW.7 asks the statement of the basis that PRIME.8 and the Effort estimate of AGENT already require, naming the records it uses; STND.CREW.12 asks that the brief that AGENT.11 requires also name the role and the depth; STND.CREW.13 asks one record, its machine sentence a recommendation; STND.CREW.23 asks the separation that PRIME.11 already makes, retrieved content reaching the work only as data, at the cost of a fresh context, which counts as a new session; STND.CREW.14 applies the check of AGENT.12 and the marking of AGENT.3 to the results of delegation taken together, which GUARD.12 makes the directing agent answer for, and carries up the referrals that PRIME.14 already requires; STND.CREW.21 asks the referral that PRIME.8 and GUARD.15 already ask, and one that the human owner's own discipline asks; STND.CREW.20 and STND.CREW.22 are recommendations within provisions that instantiate AGENT.10 and GUARD.14. Their cost is a statement, a record, or a referral, so binding them at every class raises no floor (GOV.11); the judgment is the custodian's, recorded here (PRIN.10).

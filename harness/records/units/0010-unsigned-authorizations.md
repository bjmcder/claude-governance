# Unit 0010: unsigned authorizations

The security verification of unit 0008 (`review-records/round-2026-10-24/harn-auth/verify-security.md`, M-c) found that a signature is required for an authorization only where the work is assured or the corpus holds a signer register. Without a register, an agent is able to append to the ledger an unsigned `authorize` that names the human owner as actor, and to recompute the chain, since appends are permitted writes; `environment --authorization ID` then accepts it, and the printed summary is the only control. The custodian directed on 2026-10-24 that this be closed now, as a small unit. This unit refuses the derivation in that case unless the owner states, by an option, that it accepts the authorizations unsigned. The other residual of M-c, a default expiry horizon (M-b of `OPEN-ITEMS.md`), is not in scope.

## Definition of done

1. `environment` takes `--accept-unsigned`. Where `--authorization` names an authorization, the work is not assured, and the corpus holds no signer register, the derivation without the option exits 1, writes nothing, and prints the reason, beside every other reason of STND.HARN.29; it raises no uncaught exception.
2. There, with the option, the derivation proceeds as before, and the summary prints, for each authorization relied on, a line that it was accepted unsigned.
3. Where the work is assured or a signer register exists, a signature that the register verifies is required as before; the option has no effect, and the reason of a refusal says so.
4. Each criterion has a test; every earlier test passes; ruff at 79 columns adds no finding on the changed files.
5. The README and decision 0002 record the change, and this record states whether STND.HARN.29 needs an amendment.

### STND.HARN.29, revised proposal for the custodian

Superseded by the proposal in the section Revision after verification below. The decision above stands: the code conforms because it is stricter and fails closed, and the provision should still state the rule. Following the review, the statement is the derivation's and not a test of the authorization, so it moves out of the table into the body of the provision. The row Signature takes only the M1 case. A minor amendment (GOV.41), not made here:

- Row Signature, replacing its cell: "Where the work is assured, or the corpus or its ledger records a signer register, that transaction carries a signature that STND.HARN.11 accepts".
- A third sentence of the body, after the two that stand: "Where the work is not assured and neither the corpus nor its ledger records a signer register, a derived configuration SHALL rely on no unsigned authorization that the human owner has not accepted unsigned at the derivation, and SHALL name each one so accepted in its coverage report (STND.HARN.19, PRIME.7)." The condition comes first and the sentence has no trailing condition. With it the provision holds four modals, not three, so g.18 has no bundle of three to review.
- Notes, after the sentence on the row Signature: "Without a signer register, an agent able to append to the ledger is able to append an unsigned authorize that names the human owner. The row Record then does not show who granted it, and the human owner's acceptance at the derivation does (PRIME.7). A register that the ledger records and the corpus lacks was removed, so its absence lifts no signature; the ledger records one where an entry carries a signature or a transaction's scope holds the register." The floor argument that closes the Notes would name the acceptance among the costs.
- Revision row: "Minor amendment (GOV.41): STND.HARN.29: a signature is also required where the ledger records a signer register; where the work is not assured and no signer register is recorded, a derived configuration relies on an unsigned authorization only once the human owner accepts it at the derivation, and names it in its coverage report; the Notes give the reasons."
- STND.DRAFT.42 applies, since STND.HARN is LVL2: such a provision asks for no decision of the human owner other than an assigned one. The acceptance is a decision of the human owner by the term of STND.DRAFT ("an adoption, authorization, approval, ruling, or other act"). My reading is that it is assigned. GUARD.10 has an authorization granted only by the human owner and recorded, and PRIME.7 bars an agent from granting one for itself. Where nothing in the record shows the grantor, the acceptance is the human owner's own statement that the recorded grant is the owner's. It is part of the grant that LVL0 reserves to the human owner, not a new decision. It is made once per derivation, within the owner's configure act (STND.DFLT.22), not per write, so the second sentence of STND.DRAFT.42 (accept a standing authorization) holds. The owner also avoids it entirely by founding a signer register. If the custodian reads it instead as a further approval that LVL0 does not assign, two alternatives meet STND.DRAFT.42. One is to require a signature always, which raises the cost of every derivation in a corpus without a register. The other is to leave the provision as adopted and keep the refusal as this harness's stricter reading.

## Revision after verification

The cold verification of commit 4712619 (`review-records/round-2026-10-24/harn-auth/verify-unit-0010.md`) found M2, L1, and L2 fixed. It found one new Medium (N1), a residual of M1, and Lows N2 to N4, and it reworded the proposal on STND.HARN.29. This section supersedes the statements above where they differ.

- N1: `register_recorded` iterated `scope` before any check, so a ledger entry whose scope was a number or a boolean ended the command with a `TypeError`, where eceabea exited 1 with a reason. I chose to make the detection total rather than run it after the chain check. The command needs the trace to set `signed` before `read_authorizations` runs the chain check for each authorization. Running the chain check first in the command would duplicate it. A broken chain refuses anyway, so the trace need not wait for one.
  - `ledger_shape` (`derive/environment/authorizations.py`) names the first entry that is no object, whose scope is neither absent nor a list of strings, or whose signature is no text.
  - `register_trace`, which replaces `register_recorded` and returns the reason or None, counts such a ledger as a trace (fail closed).
  - `read_authorizations` refuses such a ledger with the reason before reading it further.
  - The command does not run `ledger_problems` on it.
  - A recomputed chain around such an entry, which would have reached `covers` with a non-list scope since unit 0008, is refused the same way.
  - Tests: `test_malformed_ledger_is_total` (an entry `5`, a scope `5`, `true`, `1.5`, `"SIGNR"`, `["SIGNR", 3]`, and a signature `7`); `test_command_malformed_ledger` (exit 1 with the reason, nothing written, without the option, with it, and with a register present).
- M1 residual: an agent able to rewrite the ledger from the founding entry on, with the chain recomputed, and to remove `SIGNR/`, hid the register from the ledger test. `authorized` (`cli/environment.py`) now reads the signer configuration once. Where it names an anchor key and the corpus holds no `SIGNR`, `register_trace` returns that reason, `signed` holds, `verified` is false, and the option has no effect. The configuration lies under `/etc`, where `read_config` takes it only from a file the running account is unable to alter. The ledger test stays as a second signal. Test: `test_command_anchor_without_register`, with `read_config` patched to an anchor. The command tests that expect no anchor now patch `read_config` to `SignerConfig()` (`no_anchor`), so that they do not depend on `/etc` of the machine.
- What remains where no anchor is configured: the ledger test alone is not tamper-proof. An agent that rewrites the ledger from the founding entry on, with the chain recomputed, removes `SIGNR/`, and appends an unsigned `authorize` is not detected. The owner's `--accept-unsigned` then accepts it, and the summary, the coverage report, and the draft entry naming it are the only signs. A register founded through this harness is verified against an anchor (`check_signatures` refuses a founding not signed by it). So the gap is confined to a derivation on a machine whose signer configuration names no anchor, or is alterable by the running account and so not read.
- N2, the order of checks: the trace (anchor, then shape, then signatures, then scopes) is read in `authorized` before `read_authorizations`. That function checks the shape, then for each authorization the chain (`record_problems`), then the signature. The trace does not rely on an intact chain.
- N3: once any entry carries a non-empty signature, even a blank one, the ledger shows a register for good. An agent can therefore block every unsigned derivation until the owner founds a register. This fails closed and is a nuisance only.
- N4: the trace matches a scope path whose first part is `SIGNR`, as `check_signatures` and `covers` read scopes. Spellings such as `/SIGNR` or `a/../SIGNR` are not matched; the founding entry is written by the tool.
- Mutations, each checked by patching it in turn: ignoring the anchor fails `test_command_anchor_without_register`. Dropping the shape check from the trace fails `test_malformed_ledger_is_total` and `test_command_malformed_ledger` (with a `TypeError`). Dropping it from `read_authorizations` fails both. Dropping it from the command's signature check fails `test_command_malformed_ledger` (a register present).

### STND.HARN.29 and a new STND.HARN.30, proposal for the custodian

This replaces the proposals above. STND.HARN.29 keeps its two modals and changes one row. The statement of the human owner moves to a provision of its own. A minor amendment of STND.HARN (GOV.41), not made here:

- STND.HARN.29, row Signature, replacing its cell: "Where the work is assured, or the corpus or its ledger records a signer register, that transaction carries a signature that STND.HARN.11 accepts".
- New provision STND.HARN.30, "Unsigned reliance accepted", section Environment, binding. `instantiates: ["GUARD.10", "PRIME.7"]`; `refers_to: ["STND.HARN.29", "STND.HARN.19"]`; `checked_by: ["review"]`. Body, two sentences, one modal each, conditions first:
  "Where the work is not assured and neither the corpus nor its ledger records a signer register, a derived configuration SHALL rely on an unsigned authorization under STND.HARN.29 only after the human owner accepts it at the derivation (GUARD.10, PRIME.7). Where a derived configuration relies on an unsigned authorization, its coverage report SHALL name that authorization (STND.HARN.19)."
  Every identifier it cites is in `instantiates` or `refers_to`. The manifest of STND/HARN would gain its row.
- Notes, after the sentence on the row Signature: "Without a signer register, an agent able to append to the ledger is able to append an unsigned authorize that names the human owner. The row Record of STND.HARN.29 then does not show who granted the authorization, so STND.HARN.30 asks for the human owner's acceptance at the derivation (PRIME.7). Where an entry carries a signature or a scope holds the register, the ledger records one. A register that the ledger records and the corpus lacks was removed, and its absence lifts no signature. A ledger that an agent rewrites hides that record, so a harness that reads a signer configuration outside the agent's reach also takes its anchor key as a record of a register." The floor argument would add: "STND.HARN.30 binds, because it instantiates GUARD.10 and PRIME.7, and raises no floor (GOV.11): it asks of a harness a refusal and a line of the coverage report, at the cost of the human owner's acceptance once per derivation where no signer register is founded."
- Revision row: "Minor amendment (GOV.41): STND.HARN.29, row Signature: a signature is also required where the ledger records a signer register; new STND.HARN.30: where the work is not assured and no signer register is recorded, a derived configuration relies on an unsigned authorization only after the human owner accepts it at the derivation, and its coverage report names that authorization; the Notes give the reasons."
- STND.DRAFT.42, a decision for the custodian, with both readings:
  - For (this record): STND.HARN is LVL2. The acceptance restates a grant that GUARD.10 and PRIME.7 reserve to the human owner, made once per derivation within the owner's configure act (STND.DFLT.22), so it is an assigned decision. The owner avoids it by founding a signer register.
  - Against (the verifier): the second sentence of STND.DRAFT.42 has a provision accept a standing authorization (GUARD.11), yet STND.HARN.30 refuses one without a further act of the human owner. A bare flag is also no proof of that act, since an agent that runs the derivation is able to pass it. I agree that the flag authenticates nothing. What it adds is that reliance becomes a stated act, recorded in the coverage report and the configure entry, rather than a default.
  - Options: (a) adopt STND.HARN.30 on the first reading; (b) require a signature always, so that founding a register precedes any authorized remote write (the option would then go); (c) leave STND.HARN.29 as adopted and keep the refusal as this harness's stricter reading, which conforms either way. Under (a) or (c) the code stands as it is.

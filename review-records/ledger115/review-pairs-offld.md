## Method

- Read STND.HARN.28, `_doc.md` of STND.OFFLD (Purpose, Terms, Notes) and the 31 provisions, the bodies of the 94 distinct other provisions in the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES with its ASIC and FPGA sub-profiles, HW, HW.CPU, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.GPU, HW.QPU, MEAS, ML, NUMER), and the hand-check set the brief names: HW.GPU.3, HW.GPU.4; HW.3 to HW.5, HW.7 to HW.10, HW.12 to HW.19, HW.22; STND.ANNEX.6; STND.CONC.1 to STND.CONC.29 and the Terms and Notes of STND.CONC; STND.OMP.3 and STND.OMP.23 to STND.OMP.32 with the Notes of STND.OMP on class; STND.VVUQ.1, .2, .79 to .83; STND.CODE.7; STND.CXX.21 (and STND.CXX.7); NUMER.5, .11, .16, .17; RSRC.1 to RSRC.4; AGENT.39, AGENT.40. Also read the Notes of HW, HW.GPU, HW.CPU, STND.ANNEX, and STND.CLASS.10 (the table of onsets).
- Shared-matters tables read: HW.2, HW.GPU.2, HW.CPU.2, NUMER.2, and CPS.2, HDES.2, HDES.ASIC.2, HDES.FPGA.2, ML.2, MEAS.2, HW.EMBED.2, HW.ASIC.2, HW.FPGA.2, HW.QPU.2 (AGENT has no table). The rows that bear on offload are: hardware contract of an execution path (HW.4 to HW.12, HW.GPU.3), boundary contract between execution paths (HW.13, HW.14), agreement between the alternative execution paths of a component (HW.15 to HW.19), budgets of a constrained target (HW.20, HW.21), comparative performance claims (HW.22), and in NUMER the precision a result requires (NUMER.5, .12, .17), approximation disclosure (NUMER.16, .17), and tolerance basis (NUMER.11, .25). No row lists a STND.OFFLD provision. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.CPU.5, and HW.GPU.4 are in no row (Observation 6).
- Completeness of the list: I recomputed the pair set from the front matter (each OFFLD provision against each provision of AGENT, CPS, HDES*, HW*, ML, MEAS, NUMER that instantiates one of the same DSGN, SIMPL, REPRO, VV, RSRC, INTPR, PRIME, PRIN, or QA provisions). It gives the same 523 pairs; none missing, none extra. No OFFLD provision has a pair on the shared-matters basis. Eight provisions have no listed pair because no provision of a profile instantiates their bases (OFFLD.1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 19, 23); they are checked by hand below.
- Grouping: pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, VV.8, DSGN.5, DSGN.15, DSGN.4, INTPR.9, INTPR.7, REPRO.2, RSRC.1, RSRC.4) are in group rows with one reason. Every pair on the same subject (placement and fallback, protocol content, boundary contract, shared memory, delivered arithmetic, limits, records, resource use, claims of speed, tests of paths and tolerances) has its own row or a named clause in a row. Rows that combine several OFFLD provisions do so where the other provision is the same and the reason holds for each; the reason then names what differs. The table lists each pair exactly once (checked by script: 523 pairs, numbers 1-523, no gap or repeat; 66 rows).
- Tests applied. Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner. A matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, and where the other side is an LVL2 standard, is not counted as unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile does not hold (the precedent of the random-stream candidate, ledger 111 #79). I applied it in this form: a standard adds a matter when the matter is not one of the subjects of the profile provision it sits beside (as keying streams to parts of a computation is not the independence evidence of NUMER.24); extra items for a record or a report that the profile provision already asks are not a new matter.
- Every OFFLD provision is a default (SHOULD). Where a profile or LVL0 provision binds (SHALL), a conflict is recorded only if the default cannot be met together with it; a default that adds to a binding provision is not a conflict.

## Observations (not conflicts, not unowned)

1. Data race in the Notes of OFFLD.13 against the Term it imports. The Terms of OFFLD take Data race from STND.CONC, which defines it as two accesses by different tasks, one a write, that no protection orders and that are not both atomic operations. The Notes say that an atomic operation at a scope that does not include the task it communicates with "is a data race although it is atomic". Under the CONC definition two atomic accesses are never a data race. OFFLD.13 itself does not use the word, so no provision is affected, and I do not record a conflict; but the Notes and the imported Term answer the same question differently. Either say in the Notes that such an access is unordered with respect to the other task (so a non-atomic partner would race), or add a sentence to the Terms of OFFLD that widens the meaning for scoped atomics. OFFLD.30 ("races in memory local to a group") uses the term in the CONC sense.
2. Onset of OFFLD.17 sentence 2. The Notes put "the first sentence of STND.OFFLD.12, STND.OFFLD.14, STND.OFFLD.17, and STND.OFFLD.20" at CSQ2 on DSGN.5. OFFLD.14 and OFFLD.20 have one sentence; OFFLD.12 and OFFLD.17 have two. For OFFLD.12 the second sentence is given (CSQ3, a test). For OFFLD.17 the second sentence (launch configuration rests on the limits the runtime reports, DSGN.5) is in no list, so its onset is unstated; by the same reasoning it is CSQ2. Name STND.OFFLD.17 without "first sentence", or list the second sentence.
3. OFFLD.10 says memory is released "after its last use". For asynchronous work the use is not over when the call that submitted it returns; CONC.5 says "until the last use of it by that task has completed", and the Notes of OFFLD say a release before the last use is a use after release. Write "after the completion of its last use". The Terms define a Submission to include a transfer, not a release; say in the Notes that a release is not a submission, so that OFFLD.7 (no submission through an unusable context) and OFFLD.10 (release on every path, failures included) do not read as conflicting after such an error.
4. OFFLD.7 and the response of STND.ANNEX.6. The default contract allows the path to submit offloaded work again from inputs that the fault leaves unaffected. OFFLD.7 forbids a submission through a context that the documentation says an error left unusable, and forbids reliance on the memory it holds. Read together: a resubmission after such an error needs a new context (or a new process) and host inputs; the Notes say the sources disagree on whether a new context suffices and mark that unverified. Say so beside OFFLD.7, so that a project that cites the default is not left with a resubmission it cannot make.
5. OFFLD.22. (a) It speaks of use "beyond the capacity of the accelerator", and the Terms import Capacity from HW, where a limit at which hardware only slows is not a capacity. Migration only slows. The Notes on OFFLD.21 call device memory a capacity and apply HW.8, which is right for a failed allocation and is not the case that OFFLD.22 reports. Use another word in OFFLD.22, or say that the report is the visible degradation HW.8 asks if the limit is taken as a capacity. (b) HW.9 puts a result computed across an exhausted capacity in the record of the result; OFFLD.22 reports only to the operator. Add the record or cite HW.9. (c) The Notes call it the offload form of HW.CPU.5, which applies from CSQ3, while OFFLD.22 applies from CSQ1 on INTPR.7 alone; HW.CPU.5 and HW.19 were set to apply from CSQ3 although INTPR.7 applies earlier (Notes of HW and HW.CPU). Not a conflict, since the default can be met with both, but the onsets differ for forms of one rule.
6. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.CPU.5, and HW.GPU.4 are in no row of HW.2, HW.CPU.2, or HW.GPU.2 (the same finding as Observation 7 of the review of STND.OMP). OFFLD.22, OFFLD.24, OFFLD.25, and OFFLD.31 touch them. In each case the profile is the only one that addresses the matter, and OFFLD is an LVL2 standard that adds items to the record or the report that the profile provision already asks (the accelerators and settings in the record of HW.3; the migration in the report beside HW.CPU.5 and in the record of HW.GPU.4; the end of a time measurement beside the busy fraction of HW.GPU.4). I did not count them as unowned. If the tables are meant to list every provision that two documents address, HW would own them.
7. OFFLD.23 is called the offload form of CONC.18. CONC.18 has the program size its pools so that tasks computing at once do not exceed the threads the run may use; OFFLD.23 asks only a statement of how components share the memory and queues of one accelerator. The statement is weaker than the sizing, and the check of OFFLD.21 is the nearest counterpart. The Notes also map OMP.24 to OFFLD.23, but only the device-selection sentence of OMP.24 corresponds; sentence 1 of OFFLD.23 has no OpenMP form.
8. OFFLD.31. (a) The Notes list "migration of memory" among the costs of a first submission; migration of memory that exceeds the accelerator can recur on later submissions (OFFLD.22). (b) HW.22 (binding) requires a claim of speed to count data movement and dispatch. A measurement that OFFLD.31 allows to exclude migration cannot support such a claim; say so in the Notes.
9. OFFLD.28 has the tests show "the response that the hardware contract of its execution path states" for a failed allocation of accelerator memory. HW.4 asks the contract to state the response to a detected fault; an exhausted capacity is governed by HW.8 and is not a fault under the Term, and STND.ANNEX.6 has no row for a failed allocation. A project that cites the default contract has no stated response for it, only the one HW.8 requires (halt, visible degradation, or a safe state). Say "the response that HW.8 or the contract states".
10. OFFLD.17 sentence 1 has device code take the width of execution at run time or request it. OFFLD.24 allows a build to hold compiled code for named hardware models, in which the width is a constant of the build. Read literally, the first does not admit a constant fixed in the build and confirmed at run time against the model; the Notes do not say that it does.
11. OFFLD.30 and STND.OMP.32. The Notes call OMP.32 (a mapping check for data missing from a device, stale, or mapped in error) the OpenMP form of OFFLD.30, but the checks that OFFLD.30 lists are of invalid accesses, races in group-local memory, and barriers. A check for stale or missing data has no OFFLD counterpart (OFFLD.9 states the rule it would check). Either add it to OFFLD.30 or map OMP.32 to OFFLD.9. Similarly, sentence 2 of OMP.23 (the host path of an if clause stated and tested) is a form of OFFLD.1 and OFFLD.3 and is not mapped.
12. OFFLD.2 asks the run to confirm "the accelerator on which that work executed"; OMP.23 asks confirmation that the region ran on "a device" and a report of each region that ran on the host. For a run with several accelerators OFFLD.2 is the stricter. The report of a fallback is HW.19's in both.
13. Device code and CONC. (a) Sentence 2 of CONC.2 (tasks that wait on a halted task halt or are told) has no route for device threads; OFFLD.8 reaches the host. (b) CONC.10 (a bound on a wait) and CONC.13 (cancellation observed at stated points) have no facility on device in the programming models the Notes describe; a counted spin that records a failure is one way to meet the first. The defaults can be replaced by a recorded decision, as the review of STND.OMP recorded for OpenMP. (c) CONC.24 asks tests at no fewer than three numbers of tasks; OFFLD.27 sentence 2 asks more than one size of group. Both can be met. None of these is a conflict; the adopter should know the OFFLD provisions do not remove them.
14. STND.OMP does not cite STND.OFFLD. The Notes of STND.OMP do not mark OMP.23 to OMP.32 as forms of OFFLD provisions, and the mapping and the rule that each OFFLD provision takes the onset of its OpenMP form live only in the Notes of OFFLD. An amendment of STND.OMP citing STND.OFFLD in its Notes would make the mapping reciprocal.
15. The reading copy and the brief differ on the label: the reading copy and the rehearsed `_doc.md` say revision 1.0, adopted by ledger entry 116, while this review is of the draft called 0.1. The same note was made in the review of STND.OMP.
16. OFFLD.9 has the component state which copy is authoritative per phase; HW.13 has the boundary contract state ownership. One statement can serve both if the component's statement is placed in, or cited by, the boundary contract; say so in the Notes to avoid two statements that can disagree.

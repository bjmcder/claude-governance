## Scope and method

Read in full, from `rendered/`: LVL0 (Preamble, Normative Language), PRIN, PRIME, GUARD, VV, REPRO, INTPR, DSGN, SIMPL, RSRC, QA, GOV, AGENT, MNEM, KNOW, TAG and its facets, STND.DFLT, STND.CLASS, STND.PROJ, STND.INSTR, STND.THRT, LEX, and the Lexicon term files that provisions turn on (halt and refer, promotion, proportionality judgment, authorization, record, review, and others). Ran `show` on cited provisions and grep over the source files to confirm quoted text and front matter. Method: traced every act a human owner performs for a project that starts at CSQ4 or CSQ3 under the defaults and under a constitution, through ordinary agent work to a promotion, and traced each halt-and-refer path to the actor that resolves it.

## Systemic observations

Three patterns produce most of the burden at CSQ3 and CSQ4.

First, the ledger is the human owner's keyboard. GOV.36 routes every act of the human owner through a hash-chained ledger, STND.DFLT.5 bars the agent from writing an entry in the owner's name, and only authorizations have the scribe route of STND.DFLT.17. Classification, rulings, continuation of a halted unit, promotion, and release each become a hand-written JSON line; STND.CLASS.7, GOV.24, and STND.THRT.6 add occasions. Generalizing STND.DFLT.17 to every act the owner gives an agent directly (CC6) removes most of the burden without touching PRIME.1.

Second, the floor's safeguards are written for destructive remote actions but, through the defaults' terms, capture ordinary local tooling: builds and test runs are bulk actions (CC2), caches and scratch directories lie outside the working bounds (CC3), incremental edits overwrite unverified prior state (CC14), and the agent's own running cost lacks an authorized estimate (CC1).

## Systemic observations

Three patterns produce most of the findings.

First, the onset of a provision is read off the LVL1 provision it instantiates, not off what the provision asks. STND.CLASS.10 lists documents (a schema, a contract, a limit of validity) at CSQ2; the language standards apply those rows to rules of code form that cost nothing when written and a rewrite when retrofitted (CL1, CL2). Since the custodian's projects start at CSQ3 and are promoted later, the cheap form rules belong at CSQ3 and only the documents and comparisons at CSQ2. The same reading leaves correctness hazards (unwinding through foreign frames) outside CSQ3.

Second, the consolidation into STND.CODE on 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-27 stopped at the provisions that had been ruled or ratified separately. Five binding copies of STND.CODE.14, three of the handler rule, four identical naming provisions, the Python and Julia twins on randomness, tolerances, doctests, and releases, and two forms of the finite-value option remain (CL3 to CL8, CL16 to CL18, CL25). Each pair is a place where the languages will drift, and each binding copy is one more thing a CSQ4 project reads to find it adds nothing. The remedy is uniform: one provision in STND.CODE or STND.DEPS, and in each language standard only the sentence that names a construct.

Third, where the languages do differ in mode or cadence, the reason given is the history of the ruling (which language was ruled first) rather than a property of the language (CL3, CL7, CL10, CL23, CL24). The custodian's rulings should be re-asked once for every language, so that CSQ4 work in Rust, C, and C++ is not bound where the same work in Julia or Fortran is not.

On promotion: the code standards carry a transition rule only in informative Notes, and nothing names the records a CSQ4 project keeps so that a promotion can regenerate its results (CL11, CL12). The records that matter at CSQ3 (suppression reasons, decision records, lock files, toolchain revisions) are present and cheap; at CSQ4 they are all omittable. Where a promotion is known in advance, no provision lets the intended class lead the choices that are expensive to undo (edition, subset, failure channel, dependencies), and the one early choice that is possible, a high-assurance subset, brings the deviation record with it (CL26). The failure-channel defaults (STND.CXX.19, STND.RUST.16) are the exception: the CSQ3 default already matches what the severe class asks.

No provision in these ten documents makes conformance depend on the harness.

# Deep review of 2026-10-28: corpus-systems (CS)

## 1. Scope and method

I read the reading copies of STND.SV, STND.CONC, STND.OMP, STND.MPI, STND.OFFLD, STND.PERF, NUMER, HW and every HW.*, HDES and every HDES.*, CPS, MEAS, and ML in full. To check citations and onsets I also read STND.CLASS (provisions), STND.ANNEX.1 to .5, and GOV.10, GOV.11, GOV.14, GOV.19, GOV.24, GOV.25, GOV.50, QA.10 to QA.12, VV.3, INTPR.6, INTPR.7, PRIME.13, REPRO.7, and LEX.operator through `govharness show`. I took the class onset of each provision from its `instantiates` list in the source front matter against the rows of STND.CLASS.10, and compared that onset with what each document's Notes claim. I scanned every provision body in my documents for versions, products, and vendors. I checked the cited research paths on disk. I weighed each finding by its effect on CSQ3 and CSQ4 work, on promotion from those classes, and on design choices made at CSQ3 with a later class in view.

## 3. Systemic observations

The class onset decides what CSQ3 work owes, yet it lives only in informative Notes. It also follows two rules, and the standards add case-by-case readings on top of them (CS1, CS4 to CS6). The profiles deliberately pull a whole provision down to the least severe onset among its citations, and that rule is what puts operator reports (CS8) and hardware contracts (CS2) at CSQ3. A front-matter `onset` field would make every onset checkable and remove roughly 4,000 words of Notes.

The parallel-programming standards agree in substance with HW. Their overlap comes from STND.MPI predating STND.CONC and never being reduced to MPI forms (CS7). HW.3, STND.OMP.3, STND.MPI.2, and STND.OFFLD.25 split one run record across four provisions. They are consistent, and they are cheap at CSQ3, which suits later promotion.

Promotion is the weak point. CSQ4 omits every record above the floor, and GOV.14 grandfathers nothing. Computational results can be regenerated, but measured observations, quantum-processor jobs, numerical specifications recorded before results, and checks tied to events cannot (CS3, CS10, CS11). STND.PERF.23 handles a short history well, and other documents could follow its pattern. No document lets a known future class shape design without imposing its records early (CS12).

The provisions name no tools, vendors, or versions. Only the STND.OMP Notes carry specification revisions (CS15). Many Notes cite research files outside `harness/` and narrate how those files were retrieved. A project that installs only the harness cannot follow those citations.

Every decision of the human owner in these documents traces to the floor (GOV.24 and GOV.50, PRIME.6 to .8, and PRIME.13), and no provision makes conformance depend on running the harness.

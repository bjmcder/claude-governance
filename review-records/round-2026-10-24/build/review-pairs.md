## Method

- Checks by script. Every provision body of the 26 files appears verbatim in `draft-0.1.md`, and every "Instantiates" line equals the `instantiates` field of its file. All 26 are mode `default`, use SHOULD or SHOULD NOT only, and none uses SHALL or MUST. I recomputed the pair set from the front matter (each BUILD provision against each provision of AGENT, CPS, HDES, HDES.ASIC, HDES.FPGA, HW, HW.ASIC, HW.CPU, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.GPU, HW.QPU, MEAS, ML, and NUMER that instantiates one of the same LVL0 provisions, GOV and PRIN excluded): 314 pairs, none missing, none extra. The table below was generated from the rows and checked: every pair number 1 to 314 appears once, in 59 rows, and no row names a pair that is not on the list.
- Read: STND.HARN.28; `_doc.md` and the 26 provisions of STND.BUILD; the bodies of the 87 distinct other provisions on the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES with its ASIC and FPGA sub-profiles, HW with ASIC, CPU, EMBED, FPGA, and QPU, MEAS, ML, NUMER); the LVL0 bases (REPRO.1 to .4, .7, .9, .10, SIMPL.6, DSGN.3, .8, .16, .17, VV.1, VV.8, QA.4, .7, .13, INTPR.10, RSRC.4, .7, .12, PRIME.7, .10, GUARD.5, .6, .17); STND.CLASS.10; and the hand-check set of the task (below).
- Shared-matters tables read: provision 2 of HW, NUMER, HDES, HDES.ASIC, HDES.FPGA, HW.ASIC, HW.CPU, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.GPU, HW.QPU, CPS, ML, MEAS (AGENT has none). No row lists a STND.BUILD provision, so no pair arises on the shared-matters basis; every pair arises from a common LVL0 basis. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.GPU.4, HW.EMBED.5, and HW.QPU.3 are in no row (Observation 7).
- Grouping. Pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, VV.8, INTPR.10, REPRO.1) are in group rows with one reason. A pair on the same subject (settings and delivered arithmetic, the build record against the record of a result, declared variation, the link-step setting against the floating-point control state, the design record against the build record) has its own row or a named clause. A row that names several BUILD provisions does so where the other provision is the same and the reason holds for each; where it differs, the reason says so. The BUILD column of a row lists only the BUILD provisions that are paired with the provisions named.
- Tests. Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner. A matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, with an LVL2 standard on the other side, is not counted as unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile provision does not hold (the precedent of ledger 111 #79 and of the review of STND.OFFLD, ledger 115). A default that adds to a binding provision is not a conflict; a default that cannot be met together with a binding provision is.
- Every BUILD provision is a default. The hand checks and the observations come after the table. Findings are ranked Defect (a broken rule or a factual error), Should-fix, Optional, each with a fix.

## Conflicts found by the hand checks

None of the 314 listed pairs conflicts. The four items below conflict with an LVL0 or LVL2 provision outside the list, as written. Each is closed by one clause.

- C3 (Defect). STND.BUILD.25, Environment row, asks "Each variable of the environment that a build step read" in a record written with the outputs, and the Notes say the record can be read by an investigator. A build step that reads a variable holding a credential or a token puts a secret into the record, which PRIME.10 (binding) forbids ("environment captures contain no secrets"). The same row cannot be met by the name alone on one reading and by the value on another; the provision does not say. STND.BUILD.23 puts the same variables into a cache key, which a shared cache stores. Fix: in the row, "each variable of the environment that a build step read, with its value, other than a variable that holds a secret, which the record names without its value (PRIME.10)", and for BUILD.23 "the digest of the value of each variable". The record then also meets the custodian's rule that nothing disclose a secret.
- C1 (Should-fix). STND.BUILD.5 ("fail on each difference from the file held") and STND.BUILD.20 sentence 2 ("fail on each difference from the file in the archive") count every difference as a failure. REPRO.3 accepts declared variation and STND.VVUQ.82 sentence 1 says a difference with stated sources within the agreed bound is no defect; the Notes of BUILD say the same for the output of a tool that embeds an identifier of its own run, which is the usual difference of a regenerated file. As written, a regenerated file with a declared variation fails the check. Fix: "fail on each difference that the build record does not declare as variation (REPRO.3)".
- C2 (Should-fix). STND.BUILD.14, 15, 16, 17, and 18 each say how to remove a source of difference (instant, order, locale and time zone, path, randomness) and give no alternative. REPRO.3 gives three options, STND.VVUQ.82 sentence 2 says a change SHOULD NOT be made only to remove a declared difference unless an acceptance criterion or a provision of the class requires bitwise identity, and the custodian's direction is that strict determinism is an option and not a requirement. BUILD.18 asks more than DSGN.17, which asks for a seedable source "wherever reproducibility requires". The Notes say bitwise identity is an option, yet the provisions read as unconditional. Fix: lead BUILD.14 (or add one sentence to each) with "Where the acceptance criterion or the build record does not accept the variation, ...", or "unless the build record declares the variation (REPRO.3)", and write BUILD.18 "wherever reproducibility requires".
- C4 (Should-fix, a conflict only if "discard" means delete). STND.BUILD.24 sentence 2: "the work SHOULD discard that cache whole". A cache that served a wrong output is also the evidence by which QA.13 identifies what relied on it; deleting it is the destruction of stored state without a verified recoverable prior state, which GUARD.5 calls irrecoverable and PRIME.6 reserves to the human owner, and the custodian's rule is to archive first. Fix: "SHOULD stop reading from that cache and keep it until the outputs that relied on it are identified (QA.13), then build again each output that it served and on which a result still relies".

## Observations (not conflicts, not unowned)

Ranks: S is Should-fix, O is Optional.

1. (S) Declared variation, scope and extent. The Declared variation row of the build record reads "Each output that differs between clean builds from the same declared inputs, with its source". BUILD.3 lets the build record declare variation for incremental and parallel builds against a clean serial build, which the row does not cover. REPRO.3 asks "which outputs vary, by how much, and why", and the Notes say that "a statement of each output that differs, with its source, meets REPRO.3"; the extent is missing, so the Notes overstate. Fix: "Each output that differs between builds from the same declared inputs, with its source and its extent".
2. (S) Two statements in the Notes about provisions of other documents. "STND.VVUQ.83 keeps an agent from extending a unit of work only to make builds identical": sentence 2 of VVUQ.83 lists threads, processes, and hardware models, not builds; cite sentence 1. "as STND.VVUQ.82 treats a true race": VVUQ.82 does not mention a race; say that an undeclared source is a defect under REPRO.3. Fix both in the Notes.
3. (S) BUILD.11 and BUILD.12 against STND.CXX.4 and STND.CXX.25. They differ in what they ask of the same C++ build (hand checks). The Notes say CXX.4 holds "the form of the first sentence of BUILD.11 for C++". It holds that form only in part (no default value, no "one place", no link step). Fix: say in the Notes that BUILD.11 is the wider, or amend CXX.4 to cite BUILD.11 and add only the C++ items, and CXX.25 sentence 2 to cite BUILD.12 (GOV.31).
4. (O) A declared variation of a numerical build output that is judged by a tolerance needs a basis under NUMER.11; BUILD says nothing of a tolerance. Say in the Notes that outputs compared bitwise need none and that a tolerance on a numerical output rests on NUMER.11.
5. (O) The Notes say that "a result traces through the integrity digest of the output that computed it to the build record". No provision asks a result record to hold that digest or cite the build record, and HW.3 does not. REPRO.1 asks the identity of the environment; say that, or soften the sentence. Not counted as an unowned matter (only HW addresses the path record, and the link is LVL0's).
6. (S) STND.BUILD.6 and STND.BUILD.4 and 5. BUILD.6 has each build write its generated files only into its build tree. BUILD.4 sentence 2 has a generated file changed only by running its generator, and BUILD.5 covers a generated file that the version history holds; updating such a file writes into the source tree. As written, BUILD.6 forbids it. Fix: "other than the update of a generated file that the version history holds, by running its generator (BUILD.4)".
7. (O) HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.GPU.4, HW.EMBED.5, and HW.QPU.3 are in no row of the shared-matters tables (the same finding as Observation 6 of the review of STND.OFFLD). BUILD.25 and BUILD.11 touch them. In each case the profile alone addresses the matter, and BUILD adds items to a record that the profile provision already asks; I did not count them as unowned.
8. (O) Hardware realizations. A transformation step that produces a realization (HDES.3) is a build step in the sense of REPRO.1, and the tool, settings, and seeds sit in the design record. The Purpose of BUILD says software. Say in the Notes that the design record is the build record of a realization (HDES.2 row 1), so that two records of one step do not arise.
9. (O) HW.5 has the contract state the settings that set delivered arithmetic; BUILD.11 has the repository configuration state them. One statement can serve both if the contract cites the repository configuration; say so in the Notes, to avoid two statements that can disagree.
10. (S) STND.BUILD.10. Sentence 2 stops a build whose repository configuration states no hardware model. The Notes say that a set taken from the build host "is a statement under STND.BUILD.10", and the Hosts row of BUILD.25 has a case for it. As written, such a configuration states no hardware model and the build stops. Fix: "states no hardware model and no rule that takes the set from the build host". Also, "hardware model" has the meaning of HW (the design and revision of a device); a target of device code is often a family of such models. State that in the Notes, as STND.OFFLD.24 does not.
11. (S) STND.BUILD.26 sentence 2 and STND.PY.14. See the hand checks. Fix: say that a declared range whose end points are checked states its interior once as unchecked, or that a range checked at its end points needs no further statement.
12. (O) The Term Checking build is defined against "the builds from which the work reports results". A test verdict of a checking build is a result of the checks of the change (CXX.26), and not a result in the sense of the Notes. Say so in the Term or the Notes.
13. (O) The Notes say the standard "states each outcome so that it holds for a compiled and for an interpreted language alike". BUILD.11 and the Settings row speak of compilation units and link steps. Say that the settings of a run of an interpreted work belong to the standard of the language (STND.JL.5), or let the Term Compilation unit include what an interpreter loads.
14. (O) BUILD.5 asks the checks of each change to regenerate each held generated file. Where regeneration is expensive, RSRC.12 lets it wait for the release; say so in the Notes, as for BUILD.19.
15. (O) The scope of "variable of the environment" differs: BUILD.1 (that the step reads and that can change an output), BUILD.23 (that the step reads), BUILD.25 (that a step read). Use one scope, or say why the cache key and the record are wider.
16. (O) The Term Integrity digest (DEPS) is a hash of a component, recorded to check a later copy. BUILD.23 and BUILD.25 use it for inputs and outputs of builds. Say in the Terms that the use is wider, and whether an output served by a shared cache from outside the provisioned environment is a fetched component under DEPS.6.
17. (O) BUILD.22 applies to a file that "the repository configuration marks as test data"; nothing asks the marking. Say in the Notes that the provision has effect only where the marking exists, or ask it.
18. (O) STND.DEPS.8 is mode default and its body uses SHALL, which BUILD.25 relies on. Outside this draft.
19. (O) CODE.4 and BUILD.12: a finding suppressed at its location with its reason is not reported and so does not fail the checks. One clause in the Notes.

# STND.HARN.28 pair review: STND.STDC (2026-10-24)

Scope: the 354 pairs of `review-records/round-2026-10-24/c/pairs.md`, read against the reading copy `draft-0.1.md` and the rehearsed copy `REPO/.scratch/c-draft/rehearsed/governance` (STND/STDC 1.md to 40.md and `_doc.md`). The reading copy was compared with the 40 provision files by script: every provision body appears verbatim in the reading copy, every "Instantiates" line equals the `instantiates` field of its file, and the ancestors named in parentheses in each body equal that field. The rehearsed copy has STND.CXX at revision 1.0 (CXX.23 still instantiates HW.11), while the draft is written against STND.CXX 1.1 (Observation 1). The reading copy calls itself revision 1.0, adopted in ledger entry 118, while this review is of the draft called 0.1; the same label difference was noted in the review of STND.OFFLD.

## Method

- Read STND.HARN.28 as the earlier reviews applied it, the Purpose, Terms, and Notes of STND.STDC, its 40 provisions, the bodies of the 104 distinct other provisions in the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES with its ASIC and FPGA sub-profiles, HW, HW.ASIC, HW.CPU, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.GPU, HW.QPU, MEAS, ML, NUMER), and the hand-check set: STND.CXX 1.0 in full (and the pending 1.1 through `amendments/STND.CXX.diff`), STND.PY and STND.JL on failures, ignored results, arithmetic, foreign interfaces, and checks (STND.PY.22, .25 to .31; STND.JL.13 to .21), STND.CODE.1 to .13, STND.CONC.1 to .29 with its Terms and Notes, STND.DEPS.1 to .11, NUMER.5, .16, .17, HW.CPU.3, STND.MPI.3 to .25 and STND.OMP.5 to .33 where they bind C, PRIME.4, PRIME.2, GOV.11, GOV.18, GOV.19, GOV.31, GOV.48, and the class table STND.CLASS.10. The pending amendments of NUMER, STND.CONC, STND.PY, STND.JL, and STND.OMP were read in diff form; none changes a provision this review turns on.
- Shared-matters tables read: provision 2 of HW, NUMER, CPS, HDES, ML, MEAS, HW.CPU, HW.GPU, and HW.EMBED (HDES.ASIC, HDES.FPGA, HW.ASIC, HW.FPGA, and HW.QPU follow the same shape). The rows that bear on STND.STDC are: hardware contract of an execution path (HW.4 to HW.12, HW.CPU.3, HW.GPU.3, HW.EMBED.3, .4, .6), boundary contract between execution paths (HW.13, HW.14), agreement between alternative paths (HW.15 to HW.19), budgets of a constrained target (HW.20, HW.EMBED.3), comparative performance claims (HW.22); in NUMER the precision a result requires (NUMER.5, .12, .17), approximation disclosure (NUMER.16, .17), and tolerance basis (NUMER.11, .25); in CPS the commands to hardware (CPS.5 to CPS.7) and the behavior of a control loop (CPS.8, CPS.9). No row lists a provision of STND.STDC. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, and HW.GPU.4 are in no row (Observation 6).
- Completeness of the list: I recomputed the pair set from the front matter (each provision of STND.STDC against each provision of AGENT, CPS, HDES*, HW*, MEAS, ML, NUMER that instantiates one of the same ancestors). It gives the same 354 pairs with the same bases; none missing, none extra. Eighteen provisions have no listed pair because no provision of a profile instantiates their bases (STND.STDC.3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 14, 15, 17, 19, 24, 31, 32, 33, 35, 38, 39); they are checked by hand below. STND.STDC.2 has no pair with CPS.5 or CPS.9 although the C reading of CXX.18 that it applies concerns them; I checked that pair by hand.
- Grouping: pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, DSGN.2, DSGN.4, DSGN.5, DSGN.15, INTPR.10, REPRO.2, REPRO.3, RSRC.4) are in group rows with one reason. Every pair on the same subject (delivered arithmetic and options, boundary contract, memory model of shared state, budgets of a constrained target, approximation and tolerance, records of a result, command limits and plausibility checks, failure responses) has its own row or a named clause in a row. Rows that combine several provisions of STND.STDC do so where the other provision is the same and the reason holds for each; the reason then names what differs. The table lists each pair exactly once (checked by script: 354 pairs, numbers 1 to 354, no gap or repeat; 89 rows).
- Tests applied. Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner. A matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, and where the other side is an LVL2 standard, is not counted as unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile does not hold (the precedent of ledger 111 #79, applied as in the review of STND.OFFLD).
- STND.STDC.1 and STND.STDC.2 apply provisions of STND.CXX; where a pair is with the C reading of one of those provisions I judged the provision of STND.CXX as written for C++ and then in its C reading. Where a profile provision binds (SHALL) and the provision of STND.STDC is a default, a conflict is recorded only if the default cannot be met together with it.

## Observations (not conflicts, not unowned)

1. STND.STDC depends on STND.CXX 1.1, which is pending. The front matter of STND.STDC.1 lists no HW.11, which matches CXX.23 in 1.1 (it instantiates DSGN.15 alone) and not in 1.0 (DSGN.15 and HW.11); the CXX.15 row says nothing of concurrent calls, which CXX.15 in 1.1 leaves to STND.CONC.6; the Notes describe CXX.23 as 1.1 does. The rehearsed copy has CXX at 1.0 (CXX.23 instantiates HW.11), so the rehearsal and the pair list did not test the draft against 1.1. The draft and `amendments/STND.CXX-draft.md` both name ledger entry 118. The lead sets the entry numbers and the order at the coalesce; the order has to put CXX 1.1 first (Should-fix 11).
2. The list of responses to a listed failure in CXX.16 (and so in the C reading, STND.PY.22, STND.JL.15) includes returning the condition in the result, while HW.8 and HW.GPU.3 name halt, visible degradation, or a safe state. A function that returns the condition is a layer of a path whose top responds as HW.8 requires, so the two are met together. The question is inherited from STND.CXX and is not made worse for C. A cross-reference in the Notes of STND.CXX.16 would settle it for the three languages; not an item for STND.STDC alone.
3. STND.STDC.40 (state means and soundness of a verification that finds C code free of a kind of undefined behavior) and STND.CONC.27 (state method and what it does not cover for a protocol of concurrent tasks) address the same record when the verification concerns data races. One statement serves both; saying so in the Notes of STND.STDC.40 would avoid two that disagree.
4. The floor argument for STND.STDC.29 rests on reading a failed write as a failure of a kind that PRIME.4 lists. The Notes assert that reading ('a failed write of a result among them') and also put the floor argument before the custodian. Put as the question it is: does 'resource exhaustion' in PRIME.4 reach an I/O error that is not a full volume? If not, STND.CODE.7 does not already ask the test, and STND.STDC.29 tightens PRIME.4 by one look at one location. The Notes of STND.CXX do not discuss it for C++.
5. Headers that C and C++ units both include are C source and C++ source (Term). Both readings then apply to one declaration. The ones that can differ are STDC.23 (a constant object in a header has external linkage in C and internal linkage in C++, and an inline function has different linkage rules), STDC.7 against CXX.6 (CXX.6 exempts a foreign interface, which `extern "C"` declarations are), and STDC.37 against CXX.15. The draft has no provision on the guard itself (C linkage in C++), which belongs to CXX.20 to CXX.22 and is not applied. No conflict follows, since a macro or a declaration in the common subset meets both readings; the Notes could say so in one sentence.
6. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, and HW.GPU.4 are in no row of HW.2, HW.CPU.2, or HW.GPU.2 (the same finding as Observation 6 of the review of STND.OFFLD). STND.STDC.1 touches them through CXX.4, CXX.9, and CXX.27. Only HW addresses each matter and the rows add no item, so I did not count them as unowned.
7. STND.PY.31 and STND.JL.18 ask the reason at the point of use of an option or a construct that relaxes a check or an assumption; the C reading of CXX.4 and CXX.24 asks the repository configuration to state the option and a check to precede an assumption, with no reason at the use. NUMER.16 and NUMER.17 carry the disclosure. A difference of form between the sibling standards; the adopter may want the same form in all.
8. The label difference between the reading copy (revision 1.0, entry 118) and the draft (0.1) is the one noted in the reviews of STND.OMP and STND.OFFLD.
9. CXX.22 binds the C++ side of a boundary: a C++ exception raised in code that a foreign interface invokes is caught within that code. In a project that has both languages, the C side has no matching obligation in STND.STDC (c1 leaves it unverified whether the ISO C standard defines a nonlocal jump across foreign frames; STND.OMP.21 covers the structured block). The Notes say as much. Recorded so the adopter knows the C side of such a boundary is not covered.

## Findings, ranked

### Defect (a broken rule or a factual error)

1. STND.STDC.12, second sentence: 'a left operand of unsigned type or a nonnegative value whose shifted result its type represents'. A left operand of a type narrower than int (an 8-bit or 16-bit unsigned type) is promoted to int before the shift, and a left shift that does not fit the promoted type is undefined, so 'unsigned type' admits `x << 24` on an unsigned 8-bit `x` with a count that passes the first clause. The Notes on integers say so themselves ('a narrow unsigned operand is overflowed or shifted as a signed int'), and c1 practice 8 asks that a left shift of a signed value be replaced by an operation on an unsigned value. Fix: 'a left operand whose type after the integer promotions is unsigned, or a nonnegative value whose shifted result that promoted type represents'.
2. Notes, 'Undefined behavior' paragraph: 'That is a project decision recorded in a build file, which the C reading of STND.CXX.4 states.' The third sentence of CXX.4 has the repository configuration state options that set floating-point semantics, contraction, the target instruction set, and the mode of library checks; the second disables language extensions. An option that defines signed overflow, the aliasing assumption, or the removal of null checks is none of these, so CXX.4 does not state it, and the Purpose line 'the settings that the repository configuration states ... are those of the C reading of STND.CXX.4' leaves the trigger of the second sentence of STND.STDC.16 ('where the work builds code under such an option') without a record. Fix: give the CXX.4 row of STND.STDC.1 a change, 'and each option that defines a behavior that the ISO C standard leaves undefined'; the onset (CSQ3) and the ancestors (REPRO.2, SIMPL.6, HW.5) are unchanged. Otherwise reword the Notes.

### Should-fix

1. STND.STDC.29 against STND.STDC.30 (and the CXX.25 sentence that each warning fails the checks). STDC.29 lets formatted writes to a stream go untested one by one; STDC.30 has each call of a library function that reports a failure of a listed kind be diagnosed when its result is ignored. A call to a formatted output function whose result is ignored under STDC.29 is then a failing diagnostic under CXX.25, and a cast to void is a discard that the Notes say meets neither provision. It can be met only by a suppression at the location with its reason (CODE.4), which the Notes do not name. The same friction holds for a call whose failure the response of another standard receives (MPI.5 with an error handler that ends every process). Fix: in STDC.30, 'other than a call whose failure the test of STND.STDC.29, or an error handler that ends the run, receives'; or say in the Notes that such a call carries the suppression of STND.CODE.4 with a reference to the test.
2. STND.STDC.39 onset. The Notes give CSQ2 ('takes its onset'), while STDC.39 instantiates DSGN.3 alone, which STND.CLASS.10 applies at CSQ0 for contracts of safety-related components and otherwise from CSQ3. CXX.26 is at CSQ2 because its second sentence verifies the protocol invariants of DSGN.15; STDC.39 extends only the first. Fix: CSQ3, or instantiate the verification that gives the row. The Notes also say STDC.39 'lists the kinds of STND.STDC.8 to STND.STDC.14 and STND.STDC.27'; the provision lists STDC.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, and 27 and omits STDC.13. Fix: list the numbers in the Notes.
3. Reading of one row. The Notes put CXX.24 and CXX.27 at CSQ1 on the row 'checking of postconditions and invariants' (DSGN.2), as STND.CXX does, and STDC.11, 13, 18, 21, 22, and 27 at CSQ3 because they check preconditions. CXX.27 asks precondition checks of the library. Say why the row is read one way for the rows of STDC.1 and another for the provisions of STND.STDC, or align them.
4. Term 'C source' includes external code copied into the tree. DEPS.11 keeps incorporated material apart from the work's own. Fix: add 'other than external material that the work incorporates (STND.DEPS.11)' to the Term, as the Term of 'High-assurance code' in STND.CXX excludes it; otherwise each default applies to code the work does not maintain, and the sentence 'conforms when a change next touches it' cannot be met.
5. STND.STDC.36: 'Where concurrent tasks write bit-fields of one structure'. A task that reads a bit-field while another writes an adjacent one races on the one memory location the Notes describe. Fix: 'access ..., at least one of them by a write' (the Term 'Shared mutable object').
6. STND.STDC.20: 'a string or of other data to the size of its destination'. 'Other data' reaches a narrowing of a floating-point value, whose disclosure belongs to NUMER.16 and NUMER.5 (NUMER.2). Fix: 'a string or a sequence of bytes', and say in the Notes that numerical narrowing is NUMER's.
7. CXX.7, first sentence, in C. 'A type distinct from that of a pointer into host memory' is not met by a type alias in C; it needs a different pointee type or a structure that wraps the pointer. The Notes say CXX.7 'holds for C as written' and speak of registers only. Fix: a change in the CXX.7 row that says so.
8. Comparisons of STDC.16 (tests also pass without the option) and STDC.26 (tests compare results with a build without the qualifier) state no tolerance. Where the results are numerical the tolerance rests on NUMER.11, and a bitwise criterion applies only where the acceptance criterion demands it (STND.VVUQ.82). Fix: 'within the tolerance of the acceptance criterion'.
9. STND.STDC.28 adds almost nothing to the C reading of CXX.16 that STDC.2 already makes binding ('errno alone' is none of its five responses). A binding provision that restates a binding one is a restatement (GOV.31) and carries a floor argument it does not need. Fix: put the sentence as a change in the CXX.16 row of STDC.2 ('A report through errno alone is not a return of the condition in the result'), or give the Notes a case that the C reading of CXX.16 does not reach.
10. Notes. (a) 'so the repository configuration names an edition by the standard it denotes and by the value of the version macro that the edition defines' is an obligation stated in the indicative, in no provision; put it in the CXX.2 or CXX.4 row, or reword it as the reason for the row. (b) 'at least one widely used compiler' is a claim of notability with no source (STND.DOCS.9); say 'a compiler' or cite c1. (c) The Notes tell how c1's sources were read ('read as extracts that a retrieval tool wrote'; 'its source S24 is excluded'), which is narration of the research; STND.DOCS asks Notes without it.
11. Order and rehearsal. STND.STDC.1 depends on STND.CXX 1.1 (Observation 1). Apply CXX 1.1 first, re-run the rehearsal of the round on top of it, and set the entry numbers apart (both name 118).

### Optional

1. STDC.8, STDC.11, STDC.12 give a closed list of ways to show an operation safe ('a check, a range argument that a comment states, or a checked operation'), with no place for the result of a static check that proves the range; JL.17 asks the check of untrusted input only. STDC.8's 'bounds invariant that a comment states' cannot serve an index from untrusted input (CODE.6, binding), and STDC.21 names 'length, count, or offset' and not 'index' (JL.13 names it).
2. STDC.24, second sentence, cannot be met for an operand of # or ##, or a parameter used as a type name; add the exceptions.
3. STDC.29: read the error indicator before the stream is closed; the stream pointer is invalid after the close.
4. STDC.25 exempts a branch for a target 'recorded as unchecked'; say that results computed on that path carry no evidence from the checks (PRIME.3, HW.EMBED.5 for the image).
5. Term 'Conditional feature': defined by a macro that 'reports absence'; some features (the bounds-checking interfaces, the floating-point annex) are reported by a macro defined when present. Say 'reports its presence or its absence'.
6. STDC.5 takes in locks (Term 'Owned resource'), which CONC.8 sentence 3 already holds; say that a lock is under CONC.8.
7. No provision asks the result of an allocation be tested before use (the commonest exhaustion in C; PRIME.4 and CXX.16 apply once detected). STDC.30 does not reach it, since the result is used. A sentence in STDC.30 or STDC.32 would close it.
8. The C reading of CXX.4: add the pragmas that set floating-point contraction and access to the floating-point environment, which are in the source and not in options. The Notes mark unverified whether C needs a pragma to access the environment; the ISO text, as I recall it, makes a change of a mode or test of a flag in a unit translated with it off undefined [not verified here]. The Notes' paraphrase of HW.CPU.3 joins confirmation and restoration. Code linked from units built with relaxed floating-point options can set the control state of the whole process at start [general knowledge, not in c1]; HW.CPU.3 confirmation covers it.
9. CXX.2 reads 'the documentation of each compiler and each standard library implementation ... states as supported'. For C the library documentation seldom names an edition; use 'of each compiler, and of each implementation of the C library that states one'.

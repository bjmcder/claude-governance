# STND.HARN.28 pair review: STND.RUST (2026-10-24)

Scope: the 282 pairs of `pairs.md`, read against the draft `draft-0.1.md` (STND.RUST 0.1, 36 provisions) and the rehearsed copy `REPO/.scratch/rust/rehearsal/` (STND/RUST 1.md to 36.md and `_doc.md`). The other documents were read from `REPO/harness/governance` and `REPO/rendered`; the rehearsed copy has the same text for them. No file other than this report was edited.

## Method

- Read: the brief (Common, Review agents); the Terms, Notes, and 36 provisions of the draft; the bodies of the 100 distinct other provisions in the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES with its ASIC and FPGA sub-profiles, HW with ASIC, CPU, EMBED, FPGA, GPU, QPU, MEAS, ML, NUMER); the LVL0 provisions that the pairs rest on (DSGN.1 to DSGN.5, DSGN.14 to DSGN.16, REPRO.1 to REPRO.3, REPRO.7, REPRO.9, SIMPL.4, SIMPL.6, SIMPL.11, VV.1, VV.8, INTPR.7, INTPR.8, INTPR.10); and the hand-check set of the task: all provisions of STND.CODE, STND.CONC, STND.DEPS, and STND.DOCS; all provisions of STND.CXX, STND.PY, and STND.JL (for unsafe code or its analog, panics and exceptions, failures, arithmetic, foreign interfaces, packages and lock files, and checks); NUMER.5, NUMER.16, NUMER.17; CPS.5, CPS.9; GOV.48; PRIME.2, PRIME.4 (and PRIME.13); STND.INSTR.10; STND.CLASS.10 and the class-onset paragraphs of the Notes of STND.CONC, STND.CXX, STND.PY, and STND.JL.
- Shared-matters tables read: HW.2, HW.CPU.2, HW.GPU.2, HW.EMBED.2, HW.ASIC.2, HW.FPGA.2, HW.QPU.2, NUMER.2, CPS.2, HDES.2, HDES.ASIC.2, HDES.FPGA.2, ML.2, MEAS.2 (AGENT.2 holds no table). No row lists a provision of STND.RUST, as for any LVL2 standard. The rows that bear on this standard are: hardware contract of an execution path (HW.4 to HW.12; HW.CPU.3, HW.GPU.3, HW.EMBED.3, HW.EMBED.4, HW.EMBED.6 as members), boundary contract between execution paths (HW.13, HW.14), agreement between the alternative execution paths of a component (HW.15 to HW.19), budgets of a constrained target (HW.20, HW.21), and in NUMER.2 the precision a result requires (NUMER.5) and approximation disclosure (NUMER.16, NUMER.17). HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.CPU.5, and HW.GPU.4 sit in no row (Observation 5).
- Completeness of the list: the pair list regenerated by `python3 -B -m govharness pairs` on the rehearsed copy is identical to `pairs.md`; I also recomputed the pairs from the front matter by my own script (each RUST provision against each adopted provision of AGENT, CPS, HDES*, HW*, MEAS, ML, NUMER that instantiates one of the same LVL0 provisions outside GOV and PRIN): 282 pairs, none missing, none extra, numbered 1 to 282 without gap. Thirteen provisions have no pair because no provision of a profile instantiates their bases (RUST.4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 17, 20, 28, 33, 34); they are checked by hand in the second part. No pair rests on a shared-matters row.
- Draft against files, by script: for each of the 36 provisions, the body appears verbatim in `draft-0.1.md`, the "Default/Binding. Instantiates ..." line equals the `mode` and `instantiates` fields of its file, every provision or standard cited in a body is in `instantiates` or `refers_to`, and every instantiated provision appears in a parenthesis of the body. No difference. The modal count per provision gives three modals for RUST.8, 12, 22, 23, 25, 26, 27, 29, 31, 33 and no other, which equals the bundle list of the Notes; `tools/findings.py` on the rehearsed copy reports only the ten g.18 notices on those bundles. Every default instantiates only DSGN, SIMPL, INTPR, REPRO, VV, or HW, as the class paragraph says.
- Grouping: pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, VV.8, DSGN.5, DSGN.15, REPRO.1, REPRO.2, REPRO.7, REPRO.9) are in group rows with one reason; the reason names what differs where it differs. Every pair on a shared subject has its own row, or a named clause in a row. The table lists each pair exactly once (checked by script on this file: 282 pair numbers, 1 to 282, no gap, no repeat; 63 rows; each row's provisions equal those of its pair numbers in `pairs.md`).
- Tests applied (as in review-pairs-offld.md). Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner; a matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, with an LVL2 standard on the other side, is not unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile does not hold. A default that adds to a binding provision is not a conflict; a binding provision that adds to a profile provision at the cost of wording or one record is not either.
- Rank of findings: Defect (a broken rule of the brief or of the corpus, or a factual error), Should-fix (a gap or ambiguity a reviewer will meet), Optional (preference).

### I. The five binding provisions

Each was tested for (a) whether it can be met together with every provision of the corpus that binds, (b) whether it asks more than PRIME, GUARD, GOV, or a floor provision already asks, at the cost of wording or one record, and (c) whether its Notes state that argument and leave the decision to the custodian without writing that the custodian has ruled.

- **RUST.7** (GOV.48, DSGN.1). (a) Meets with RUST.3 and RUST.4; a project instrument may replace them. (b) It adds to GOV.48 the writing of the argument of RUST.3 and the conditions of RUST.4 for the construct an agent writes or changes. GOV.48 does not state that duty, so the argument is the one of STND.PY.4 and STND.CXX.3: the cost is the wording of one comment at each construct. (c) The Notes argue the cost and say the question is "a decision for the custodian"; that is the right content but the wrong tense once the standard is adopted (Defect 1). The custodian ruled on 2026-10-23 that STND.PY.4 (cost: one annotation) and STND.CXX.3 raise no floor (Notes of STND.PY and STND.CXX); that ruling is not for RUST.7, but the argument is the same one.
- **RUST.14** (PRIME.4). (a) Meets with CPS.4, HW.8, HW.GPU.3, STND.CONC.1, STND.CONC.2 (pairs 12 to 14). (b) Sentence 1 lists the responses PRIME.4 admits; no addition. Sentence 2 requires that a replaced hook still deliver message and location: PRIME.4 requires an actionable diagnosis, and a hook that discards the message of a panic under the aborting strategy halts without one; "and the location" is a little more than "actionable diagnosis" when a build removes location detail (not in rs1; unverified). (c) The Notes argue it under "Floor". Gaps: the term "hook" does not cover the panic handler that a program without the standard library must define (Should-fix 1); the Python and Julia counterparts rule on logged records (Should-fix 5). A panic as a response for code that commands hardware does not by itself meet PRIME.13: under the aborting strategy the process halts with no destructor run (Observation 3); the same holds for CXX.16 ("throw") and PY.22 ("raise").
- **RUST.15** (PRIME.4). (a) Meets. (b) Adds the declaration; STND.CXX.17 is the precedent and the cost is one attribute or return type. The compiler marks a `Result` as must-use (not retrieved in rs1; unverified); the provision does not rest on it, because it names an outcome (a type or an attribute by which the compiler diagnoses an ignored call). It names only "the compiler", where STND.CXX.17 also admits a static check (Optional 1). It does not reach a failure carried as a field inside a successful result (a convergence flag in an `Ok` value); STND.CODE.7 governs the caller that reads it. (c) Argued in the Notes.
- **RUST.16** (PRIME.4, DSGN.2, CPS.5, CPS.9). (a) Meets with CPS.5, CPS.9, ML.6. (b) The provision rests on STND.CODE.6, which it narrows to two constructs, and on CPS.5 and CPS.9 as STND.CXX.18 does. The term "Untrusted input" includes the arguments of each function that library code makes visible outside its crate, unless the threat model records the caller as trusted; so a precondition check of a public function written with `debug_assert!` becomes a violation at every class, which is a code change in much library code (it is the idiom of the standard library for internal contracts, from recollection; unverified) and not "one look at one location". The same reach is in STND.PY (PY.25) and STND.JL (JL.13), and the custodian ruled on 2026-10-23 that the reach raises no floor for those two (Notes of PY and JL). That ruling is not for Rust, and the Notes of RUST say nothing of the reach (Observation 2). (c) The Notes argue the floor but not the reach. The wording is narrower than STND.CODE.6, which it should not be (Should-fix 2).
- **RUST.36** (PRIME.2). (a) Meets with AGENT.3, AGENT.4, AGENT.47, STND.CONC.27. (b) PRIME.2 forbids claiming a check that was not run and misreporting a status. A claim of soundness without a statement of means is not a misrepresentation, so sentence 1 asks for the sentence that names the means; STND.CXX.29 is the precedent, and its Notes record the custodian's ruling that it raises no floor. RUST.36 reaches every claim "that code of the work is free of undefined behavior, or that a module is a sound module", in any document, at every class; the Notes state the cost as one sentence and send the question to the custodian, which is correct and is not a statement that the custodian has ruled. (c) The tense issue of Defect 1 applies.

## Findings of the hand checks

### Defect

1. **Notes narrate the drafting and a pending decision.** The first sentence of the paragraph "Direction and sources" says what "the direction of 2026-10-24 for this standard asks for"; the paragraph "Agents and unsafe code" ends "that it raises no floor (GOV.11) is a decision for the custodian", and the paragraph "Floor" says that it "goes to the custodian as a decision". The brief says Notes carry no narration of drafting, and the sentences become false when the standard is adopted (the reading copy already says it was adopted in ledger entry 118). Fix: delete the first sentence of "Direction and sources" and keep the sources and the custodian's standing directions as statements; delete the two sentences on the decision and carry the question in the report to the custodian; after a ruling the Notes may say "the custodian ruled ... " as the Notes of STND.CXX do for CXX.29.

### Should-fix

1. **RUST.14 sentence 2 and programs without the standard library.** "The hook that runs at a panic" names a facility of the standard library. A program without it must define a panic handler, and a handler that loops or halts without a message breaks PRIME.4 and, for an embedded controller, HW.EMBED.4 and HW.8. The profile for embedded controllers is in the corpus, so the case is not remote. Fix: "the hook or handler that runs at a panic", and add to the Notes that for a controller that drives an output the handler enters the designed safe state (PRIME.13, HW.EMBED.4).
2. **RUST.16 is narrower than the provisions it instantiates.** STND.CODE.6 says a check "SHALL NOT be removable by an execution setting", STND.CXX.18 says each check "SHALL run ... in every build". RUST.16 names only an assertion that a setting removes and the overflow checks. A check written as a conditional on a configuration flag (the debug-assertion flag, a feature) is removable by a setting and is not an assertion; CODE.6 still binds, but the Rust form leaves a reviewer without the rule. Fix: "A check of untrusted input, or a check that CPS.5 or CPS.9 requires, SHALL NOT be removable by an execution setting, a `debug_assert!` and its variants and a block compiled only when debug assertions are on among them."
3. **RUST.1 tests only the oldest release.** PY.14 and JL.3 test the extremes and the release of the production configuration; HW.7 has evidence under the current settings and HW.EMBED.5 has the verified image be the image on the target. RUST.1 sentence 2 can be met by tests at the oldest release while results and releases are built by another. Fix: add "and with the release that builds each result reported and each release made" or cite STND.DEPS.8 for the release and state the rest in the Notes.
4. **RUST.32 sentence 2 forbids a check against newer revisions, and has no exception for libraries.** "Each build of the checks ... SHOULD use the revisions that the committed lock file records" excludes the non-blocking check against the newest revisions that STND.JL.3 sentence 3 and STND.PY.14 ask for, and the Notes' reason (checks of a library reproducible) is the reverse of JL.4's allowance for a package that other work imports. Fix: "Other than a check that exercises the newest revisions that the declared ranges admit, ...", and say in the Notes whether a library is meant to commit the file (the research note rs1 says "for each deliverable"; the draft says for each set of crates).
5. **No counterpart of PY.22 sentence 3 and JL.15 sentence 2.** Two of the three sister standards bind what counts as visible degradation through logging, and they answer differently from each other; RUST.14 is silent. The custodian's ruling of 2026-10-23 keeps the JL rule (Notes of STND.JL) and covers the responses of PY.22. Fix, a decision for the custodian: add "A record that library code writes through a logging facade SHALL NOT be the only visible degradation" (as PY.22) or state in the Notes that the facade of the work is not a response; and the reconciliation should settle PY against JL.
6. **The Term "Lock file" and the Notes overstate DEPS.6.** The Term says the file records "the exact revision and the integrity digest of each package"; the Notes say the digest is what STND.DEPS.6 verifies. From recollection, a package from a source with no registry checksum (a path or a repository source) has no digest in the file, and rs1 retrieved no fact on checksums (its section 10 cites only the lock file reference). Fix: "the exact revision of each package and, where its source gives one, an integrity digest", and note that DEPS.6 sentence 3 records the digest of the first fetch where the publisher gives none.
7. **A dated vendor report in the Notes.** "A vendor reported, for 2026-08-20, three packages published from a compromised account ..." is a dated, single, unverified report. The brief asks for timeless practice, no vendor names, and notes name a product only to explain a hazard; STND.DOCS.9 lists vague attribution. Fix: "A compromised publisher account has been used to add a dependency whose build script fetched and ran a payload" with the hazard and no date, or delete.
8. **RUST.25 sentence 1 says "each type that crosses a foreign interface".** A primitive type (`u32`, a raw pointer) cannot carry a representation attribute, so the sentence cannot be met as written, and an opaque handle has no layout to fix. Fix: "Each structure, union, and enumeration that crosses ... and is not opaque to the work".

### Optional

1. RUST.15 names "the compiler"; STND.CXX.17 admits "the compiler or a static check". Align, or say why not.
2. The Notes of Unsafe code say that the privacy of the language stops code outside a module from changing its private fields. A descendant module reaches the private items of its ancestors, so the module of RUST.5 should be said to include its submodules.
3. The Notes say RUST.3 "is therefore also the Rust form of what STND.CXX.24 asks". CXX.24 asks for an establishing check or recorded proof before the assumption and rests on DSGN.2 (onset CSQ1); RUST.3 asks for an argument and applies from CSQ3. Say "corresponds to" and state the difference, or align the onset.
4. RUST.11 sentence 1 says "Code SHOULD catch a panic only at ...". A test that catches a panic to assert that a function panics, as its documentation states, is not at a listed boundary; "library code" or a clause for tests.
5. RUST.20 could add the conversion between floating-point types and of a large integer to a floating-point type (precision), or the Notes could name NUMER.5 as the holder, as they do for the functions of the standard library.
6. DOCS.9: "the language offers a mode" (use "has"); six "and not" contrasts in the Notes could read as plain sentences.
7. RUST.6 sentence 2 and STND.CODE.4 sentence 3: say that an unsafe construct outside the listed modules is admitted by an entry in the list and not by a suppression at its location.
8. DEPS.8: the Notes could say that the oldest supported release of RUST.1 is not the revision recorded with a result.
9. RUST.24 sentence 1 reaches the guard of any lock (CONC.8); where no check exists for the lock of an asynchronous library, a project replaces the default by a decision. State it, or limit the sentence to guards that a check reports.
10. Bundles: RUST.27 (three separable rules), RUST.29 (three), RUST.31 (three, one a test run) are never applied apart by the Notes' statement, but a project would likely vary one rule of them (the network rule of RUST.29 sentence 3). If that is expected, split.
11. The References table lacks STND.DRAFT, which the Notes name in the paragraph "Agents and unsafe code".
12. The reading copy's first lines state adoption in ledger entry 118 and the revision history says "Adopted."; the lead sets these at the coalesce.

## Observations (not conflicts, not unowned)

1. **Cross-language differences.** Section C lists them. Those the reconciliation must know: visible degradation through logging (PY against JL, RUST silent); the strength and onset of the rule on an assumption or unchecked operation (CXX.24, JL.18, RUST.3); checks kept on in the shipped build (CXX.27 against RUST.21, JL.34); versions tested and the lock file (PY.14, JL.3, JL.4, RUST.1, RUST.32); the binding of an unchecked index (JL.13 binds, RUST.3 and CXX.6 are defaults); and the matters that only some standards hold (Observation 8).
2. **Reach of "Untrusted input".** The Term in RUST has the same reach as in PY and JL (the arguments of each function visible outside the crate, unless the threat model records otherwise). The custodian's ruling of 2026-10-23 concerned PY and JL; for RUST the question goes to the custodian, and the Notes of RUST.16 say nothing of it. The consequence in Rust is concrete: `debug_assert!` on the preconditions of a public function.
3. **Safe state and destructors.** PRIME.13 says halting never leaves hardware outside the safe state. Under the aborting strategy a panic halts at once and runs no destructor; a destructor does not run if a value is leaked or the process exits (RUST.8 states the second). A design in which the safe state of an output is set by the destructor of a handle therefore does not meet CPS.4 under abort or after a leak. No provision of RUST says that a safe state not rest on a destructor; CPS.4 verifies the safe state and HW.EMBED.4 states the output after a halt, so the matter is owned (CPS, HW) and the verification would fail. A sentence in the Notes of RUST.9 would warn the author; no new provision is needed. The same holds for CXX (destructors and `std::terminate`).
4. **Overflow in trusted arithmetic that computes a command.** RUST.19 sentence 1 reaches untrusted input. An overflow in the integer arithmetic of a control law is a defect that PRIME.4 and DSGN.2 (postconditions) cover, RUST.2 makes the overflow-check setting visible, and CPS.5 checks the result against the command limit, which a wrapped value inside the limit passes. Owned; no provision needs change. Mention in the Notes of RUST.19 if the author wants the case visible.
5. **HW.3 in no row.** RUST.2 and RUST.29 to RUST.32 supply the settings and inputs that HW.3 asks the record of a result to identify; HW.3 is in no row of HW.2 (as review-pairs-offld.md found). The matter has one profile provision and one standard that complements it; not unowned under the test.
6. **A default beside a binding rule.** RUST.31 sentence 2 (no feature that disables a check) is a default; STND.CODE.6, CPS.5, and CPS.9 bind the same outcome. A project that replaces the default by a decision still has no feature that removes a check of untrusted input, and the feature is an execution setting under the Terms of STND.CODE.
7. **Features that select an alternative implementation.** RUST.31 sentence 1 has a feature leave unchanged the behavior of each item the crate provides without it. A feature that selects an accelerated or vectorized implementation of an item gives results that agree within a tolerance, not bit for bit; that is an alternative path under HW.15 to HW.19, verified against the reference path. Sentence 1 can be met by adding the implementation as a new item with a choice at run time, or the project replaces the default by a decision (REPRO.7). Features that exclude each other (two accelerator back ends) cannot be built together under "all features" in sentence 3. Not a conflict: both are defaults replaceable by decision. Say so in the Notes.
8. **Matters in sister standards without a Rust form.** STND.CXX.7 (distinct types for accelerator and register memory); STND.CXX.28, CXX.30, CXX.31 (coding standard of high-assurance code of safety-related components, CSQ0); STND.PY.19 and STND.JL.26 (hash values and iteration order); STND.PY.15 to PY.18 and STND.JL.22 to JL.25 (generators); STND.PY.20 and STND.JL.21 (tolerances of comparisons); STND.PY.29 and STND.JL.20 (scoped numerical settings). CXX has no provision on integer overflow either. None is a conflict; each is an omission for the adopter to accept or add. The first two bear on embedded and safety-related Rust, which the corpus covers by HW.EMBED and CPS.
9. **Bundles.** The ten g.18 notices are on the provisions the Notes name, and the Notes state that none is applied apart. Optional 10 asks whether RUST.27, 29, and 31 hold separable rules.

## Summary for the adopter

- **Conflicts: none.** Of 282 pairs, no pair is one that can be met only by failing the other, and none answers one question differently as written. Compatible with a stated reason: all 282. Supportive or the same matter with one owner: pairs on HW.3, HW.5, HW.7, HW.11, HW.13, HW.14, HW.GPU.3, CPS.4, CPS.5, CPS.9, HW.CPU.3, HW.EMBED.4, HW.EMBED.5 (rows above). Beyond the list I found no conflict with STND.CODE, STND.CONC, STND.DEPS, or STND.DOCS, and none with STND.CXX, STND.PY, or STND.JL as written: the differences in Section C are between defaults, or between a binding rule and a silence, and the reconciliation must know them (Observation 1).
- **Unowned matters: none.** Candidates tested and not counted: the record of build settings (RUST.2 and HW.3, HW.3 in no row, Observation 5); overflow and saturation (HDES.14 for designs, PRIME.4 for code, RUST.19 and RUST.20 add, Observation 4); the propagation of a panic across a foreign interface (HW.13 does not hold it and no profile provision does, so RUST.26 and RUST.27 add a matter that no profile holds, which the test does not call unowned); the safe state against a destructor (CPS and HW own it, Observation 3).
- **Count covered:** 282 of 282 pairs, each exactly once, in 63 rows (checked by script). Thirteen provisions of RUST had no listed pair and are checked by hand.
- **For the custodian:** the floor argument of the five binding provisions (RUST.7, 14, 15, 16, 36), the reach of "Untrusted input" for RUST.16 (Observation 2), and Should-fix 5 (logging as visible degradation).
- **Findings:** 1 Defect, 8 Should-fix, 12 Optional.

## Method

- Mechanical checks (by script). (1) I regenerated the pair list with `python3 -B -m govharness pairs` on the rehearsed copy: it is identical to `pairs.md` (946 pairs, no line differs). (2) Each of the 26 provision bodies in `draft-0.1.md` equals the body of its file in the rehearsed copy, each "Instantiates" line equals the `instantiates` field, and every provision has mode "default"; every Terms and Notes line of the reading copy appears in `_doc.md`. (3) The table below was produced from the pair list by a script that assigns each pair to exactly one row and then re-parses this file: the numbers expand to 946 distinct numbers, 1 to 946, no gap, no repeat, and each row's stated count equals its expanded count.
- Read: STND.HARN.28 as used in `review-records/ledger115/review-pairs-offld.md`; `_doc.md` of STND.SV (Purpose, Terms, Notes) and the 26 provisions; the bodies of the 92 distinct other provisions in the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES, HDES.ASIC, HDES.FPGA, HW, HW.ASIC, HW.CPU, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.QPU, MEAS, ML, NUMER); the `_doc.md` Terms, Where-the-rest-lives table, and Notes of HDES; the class paragraphs of the Notes of HDES, HDES.FPGA, HDES.ASIC, HW, HW.FPGA, and HW.ASIC; STND.CLASS.10.
- Shared-matters tables read: HDES.2, HDES.FPGA.2, HDES.ASIC.2, HW.2, HW.FPGA.2, HW.ASIC.2, NUMER.2. No row of any of them lists a STND.SV provision, as for the other LVL2 standards. HDES.2 row 1 (design record of a realized design) lists HDES.3 to HDES.8, HDES.11, and HDES.13 only; HDES.9, HDES.10, HDES.12, HDES.14, and HDES.15 are in no row of any table (Observation 5).
- Hand-check set read: every provision of HDES, HDES.FPGA (3 to 5), HDES.ASIC (3 to 12), HW.FPGA (3, 4), HW.ASIC (3, 4); STND.CODE.1 to STND.CODE.13 with its Terms and Notes; STND.VVUQ.64, .65, .82, .83, and .1, .33, .62 for reference; STND.DEPS.8; STND.CONC.1 to STND.CONC.23 and the Terms and Notes of STND.CONC; QA.9; DSGN.14, DSGN.15, DSGN.17, SIMPL.6, REPRO.2, REPRO.3, PRIME.8, NUMER.24, NUMER.26.
- Pairs not on the list. STND.SV.4, STND.SV.9, STND.SV.14, and STND.SV.24 have no pair (no provision of a profile instantiates their bases); they are checked by hand below.
- Tests applied (those of `review-pairs-offld.md`). Conflict: can one provision be met only by failing the other, or do the two answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner. A matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, with an LVL2 standard on the other side, is not counted as unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile does not hold (the precedent of the random-stream candidate, ledger 111 #79). Every STND.SV provision is a default (SHOULD); where a profile or LVL0 provision binds, a conflict is recorded only if the default cannot be met together with it.
- Grouping. Pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, DSGN.5, INTPR.10, REPRO.2, REPRO.3, VV.3, VV.5) are in group rows with one reason; the "Shared basis" or "Share ... only" clause lists the bases of the pairs of that row. Every pair on one subject has its own row or a named clause. A group row lists the union of its STND.SV provisions and of its other provisions in the "Provisions" column; the pair numbers say which pairs the row covers.
- The table never rates a pair a conflict. Conflicts found by the hand checks are C1 to C3 below; the three pair rows that bear on them say "see" in the conflict column. The one unowned row is marked.

## Conflicts

**C1. Simulation processes and the Terms of STND.CONC (Defect, in the Notes; no provision text changes unless the fix is taken).** The Notes of STND.SV hold that a simulation race "is the form that a data race of STND.CONC takes" and, in the same paragraph, that "this standard does not make each simulation process a concurrent task of STND.CONC, whose provisions on locks, pools, and numbers of tasks have no subject in such a simulation". The Terms of STND.CONC define a concurrent task as "a thread, task, coroutine, process, ... or other sequence of execution of the work that runs, or is able to run, while another of the same run has not completed", and a data race as two accesses by different concurrent tasks, one a write, that no protection orders. Both statements cannot stand together: a data race needs concurrent tasks. Read as written, a procedure of a testbench that waits on an event or a delay while others run is a coroutine in that sense, the simulation kernel is an event loop in the sense of the Term, and a semaphore, mailbox, or fork of a testbench is a lock, a queue, and a set of tasks under STND.CONC.7, .11, .12, and .14 (the research note does not cover these constructs; this rests on my knowledge of the language and is unverified). The Notes are informative and cannot narrow a Term of another document. The result is a doubt about whether STND.CONC.3 asks a stated protection for every variable shared between two simulation processes, which the draft intends it not to ask. Fix: (a) add to the Terms of STND.SV, or to the Notes, that a simulation process is a concurrent task of STND.CONC only where the verification set starts it as a task (a thread, a forked task, a task of software that a testbench calls) and it waits on another by a lock, queue, or event; (b) say that the scheduling of the language definition under STND.SV.5 to STND.SV.8 is the stated protection of STND.CONC.3 for each variable shared among simulation processes of the design, so that CONC.3 is met without a declaration at each variable; (c) cut "whose provisions on locks, pools, and numbers of tasks have no subject". The alternative is an amendment of the Terms of STND.CONC, a decision for the custodian. Affects the pairs STND.SV.5 to 8 and 19 with HW.11 (rows marked "see H-CONC").

**C2. SV.23 and STND.VVUQ.62 on the derivation of a stream (Should-fix).** VVUQ.62 asks that random number streams derive from a recorded root seed of at least 128 bits by spawning, jump-ahead with unique indices, or counter-based keys, and bars seeding a stream with an arithmetic combination of a root seed and a worker index. SV.23 sentence 1 lets a testbench take "a seed that the testbench derives from that one" with no limit on the derivation, and it relies on the seeding that the language gives, whose seed width the Notes do not state. A testbench that adds an instance index to the recorded seed meets SV.23 and breaks VVUQ.62; a seed narrower than 128 bits cannot meet VVUQ.62 at all (the width of the language's seed is not in the research note and is unverified). Both are SHOULD, so a recorded decision resolves a real case, but the two answer one question differently as written, and neither cites the other. Fix: in SV.23 sentence 1, replace "or by a seed that the testbench derives from that one" with "or by a derivation of the kind that STND.VVUQ.62 recommends", and add to the Notes that a seed narrower than STND.VVUQ.62 asks is stated in the record as such (REPRO.7). Affects pair 772 (SV.23 / NUMER.24).

**C3. SV.8 and the declared variation of STND.VVUQ.82 (Optional).** SV.8 sentence 1 says the values and verdicts of a simulation should not depend on the order in which the simulator starts procedures, continuous assignments, and initializers at time zero. The Notes add that a simulation race is a true race and so a defect. STND.VVUQ.82 and HW.12 say a difference whose sources are all declared and which lies within the agreement of the acceptance criterion should not be treated as a defect, and REPRO.3 permits a declared variation. A dependence on start-up order that moves a value within a declared agreement (for example an analog behavioral model) is a defect under the Notes and no defect under VVUQ.82. The Notes quote the custodian's direction as applying "as it reads" but do not say how the two apply to a value. Fix: in the Notes, say that a dependence of a verdict on order is a defect, and a dependence of a value within an agreement declared before the comparison is handled under HW.12 and STND.VVUQ.82. Affects pair 943 (SV.8 / HW.12). No conflict arises for a verdict, because a changed verdict is outside every agreement.

No pair is a conflict in the sense of "cannot both be met". The two overlaps of Observations 3 and 6 can both be met.

## Observations

Numbered 1 to 12, ranked within each rank by number: Should-fix 1, 2, 3, 4, 7; Optional 5, 6, 8 to 12. The Defect (C1) and the other conflicts (C2, C3) are in the section above.

1. **Should-fix. Scope of STND.VVUQ.64, .65 and NUMER.26 for constrained-random tests.** SV.23 sentence 2 and the Notes cite VVUQ.64 and VVUQ.65 as if a constrained-random test of a deterministic design were a test of a stochastic result. VVUQ.64 and VVUQ.65 are written for stochastic results: a fresh-seed test carries a rule of repetition that is a sequential test (VVUQ.65, NUMER.26), and a coverage figure of HDES.6 is a second stopping rule for the same random runs. State in the Notes whether the draft treats the tests of a random testbench as tests of stochastic results. If it does, say that the fresh-seed regressions of a testbench meet VVUQ.65, or record the departure. If it does not, replace "a fixed-seed test of STND.VVUQ.64" in SV.23 by "a test that runs at a recorded seed".
2. **Should-fix. The class table of SV.24 is broader than SV.13.** The Selection row lists "a synthesis directive" with no limit. SV.13 bars only a directive that changes the logic built from a selection statement. Designs use directives for what HDES.11 and HDES.15 need (keeping a synchronizer or a redundant register), and every such directive would be a reported finding, to be suppressed under CODE.4 each time. Fix: "a synthesis directive on a selection statement". The Scheduling row also lists "a variable that a combinational procedure reads before it writes it", for which no provision asks a remedy; either drop it or tie it to SV.10.
3. **Should-fix. SV.10 sentence 2 and HDES.9 ask the same check at different grain.** HDES.9 lets a warning of an inferred latch be matched by a recorded rule by kind and source "so that routine warnings are dismissed once"; SV.10 asks each latch stated in the design record with its procedure. Fix: in SV.10 sentence 2, add that a rule of HDES.9 that names the procedures it covers states the latches.
4. **Should-fix. SV.17 sentence 2 and the quoted source read against HDES.12.** The Notes quote that randomized initial values are not "for detecting correct initialization". HDES.12 accepts "simulation from each of a recorded number of random states" for the defined initial state. SV.11, SV.12 sentence 2, and SV.19 lack the "where the edition in force provides" limit that SV.9 has.** The research note calls `unique` and `priority` "the language's later qualifiers", and a concurrent assertion is a construct of a later edition. A file at an edition that lacks them cannot meet SV.11 or SV.19, and SV.12 sentence 2 points at the qualifier of SV.11. Fix: add the limit to SV.11, SV.12, and SV.19, with the stated alternative for a file that lacks it (a comment that states the priority; a check in a testbench).
8. **Optional. SV.6 and the multi-port memory.** The standard asks one writing process per variable of the synthesized set. The language-form of this idiom is from my knowledge and not in the research note.

Send the file to the custodian as a draft, so that it does not read as a ruling; the brief forbids writing that the custodian has ruled what the custodian has not.
10. **Optional. SIMPL.6 supports the wider reach, and the Notes could say so, so that a reader does not take HDES.7 as the limit.
11. **Optional. SV.15 and the target property.** A statement in the design record that the target gives a power-on value is a property of the target that rests on documentation or on measurement of the device; HW.6 marks documentation-only support and HW.FPGA.4 asks device evidence for a property marked as analysis or simulation.

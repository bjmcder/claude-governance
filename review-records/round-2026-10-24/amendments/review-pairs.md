Scope: the pairs that involve a provision whose text changed in STND.OMP 1.0.1, STND.CXX 1.1, STND.PY 1.0.1, STND.JL 1.1, NUMER 1.3, and STND.CONC 1.0.1, and the 10 pairs of `NUMER.2-pairs.md` that bear on the new row of NUMER.2. ## Method

- **Which provisions changed.** I compared every provision file of STND/OMP, STND/PY, STND/CONC, STND/CXX, STND/JL, and NUMER between `base` and `full` byte for byte (`cmp`). Changed provision files: STND.CXX.15, STND.CXX.23, STND.JL.27, NUMER.2. Nothing else in these six documents differs except `_doc.md` (Purpose, Notes, References, revision history, revision number) and manifests. `work` and `full` agree on those four files.
- **Cross-check on the reading copies.** A script split each of the six `-draft.md` files at its `####` headings and diffed it against `REPO/rendered/<DOC>.md`. Changed blocks: OMP, PY, CONC: Purpose, Notes, References, Revision History and the title only. CXX: those and STND.CXX.15, STND.CXX.23. JL: those and STND.JL.27. NUMER: those and NUMER.2. The four changed bodies appear verbatim in the drafts. So STND.OMP, STND.PY, and STND.CONC change no provision, and no pair is affected for them. The Notes-only changes (citations of STND.OFFLD, STND.CONC, STND.OMP, STND.WFLOW; class and onset statements) alter no obligation, so they create no pair under STND.HARN.28.
- **Pair lists.** I regenerated the lists with `govharness pairs` on the rehearsed copies. CXX (282), JL (170), OMP (477), PY (127), CONC (354), and NUMER (3147, on `work`) equal the committed `*-pairs.md` files line for line. The lists are therefore computed on the amended front matter: STND.CXX.23 shows basis DSGN.15 alone with HW.11, and `refers_to` is not a basis, so the new citations of STND.CONC.3, .6, and .16 create no pair. In `full`, STND.WFLOW adds 106 pairs to the NUMER list and none to the set below (no WFLOW provision shares DSGN.17 with NUMER.24).
- **Selection.** From STND.CXX-pairs.md the pairs whose changed side is STND.CXX.15 or STND.CXX.23 (4); from STND.JL-pairs.md those of STND.JL.27 (3). Pair 2341 of NUMER-pairs.md (NUMER.4 / STND.CXX.15) is the same pair as CXX 81 seen from NUMER; I cover it once, under CXX 81. NUMER.2 has no pair of its own (it instantiates GOV.26 only, which STND.HARN.28 excludes, and the new matter appears in no other table). The 10 pairs are those of `NUMER.2-pairs.md`; I confirmed by script that they are exactly the rows of NUMER-pairs.md with NUMER.24 on the changed side and DSGN.17 among the bases (10 of 166 rows of NUMER.24), and that each number maps to the row shown. Total 4 + 3 + 10 = 17 pairs.
- **Tests.** As in `review-records/ledger115/review-pairs-offld.md`. Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives to an owner.
- **Read.** The bodies of every provision in the table; STND.CONC.1 to .9, .16, .21 to .23 with the Terms and Notes of STND.CONC; HW.11, HW.12; STND.CXX Notes on .15, .23, and class onset; STND.JL Notes on .27 and class onset; NUMER Notes and the revision history; the shared-matters tables HW.2, HW.CPU.2, HW.GPU.2, CPS.2, ML.2, MEAS.2, NUMER.2 (AGENT has none); and every provision of the corpus that mentions random, seed, or stream (HDES.3, HDES.12, HW.QPU.3, HW.QPU.7, ML.3, NUMER.22 to .26, STND.CODE.9, STND.VVUQ.40, .69, .76, STND.WFLOW.12, .24, STND.JL.25, .26, STND.MPI Notes).

## Hand checks

1. **STND.CXX.23 against STND.CONC.3 and HW.11.** Before: sentence 1 asked that each conflicting access by more than one thread, interrupt handler, or signal handler be ordered by an atomic operation, a synchronization facility, or a library facility with a stated ordering (DSGN.15, HW.11). Sentence 2 asked that an object shared with a handler, where no facility orders the accesses, be a lock-free atomic or a `volatile std::sig_atomic_t` (DSGN.15). After: sentence 1 is gone; sentence 2 stays and cites STND.CONC.3; the front matter instantiates DSGN.15 alone.
   - *Content kept.* STND.CONC.3 sentence 1 gives each shared mutable object one stated protection; sentence 2 has each access follow it; sentence 3, where the protection orders by synchronization, has the facility be one that the language or library memory model, or the ordering model of the hardware contract, defines as ordering (DSGN.15, HW.11). The Notes of STND.CXX (the Concurrency paragraph) name the C++ facilities for it (atomic operations and the synchronization facilities of the standard library, of the target, and of a library whose contract states its ordering; `volatile` is none). So the part of old sentence 1 with C++ content is in the Notes and the rest is STND.CONC.3. One difference: STND.CONC.3 allows other protections (confinement, immutability) that old sentence 1 did not name; this loosens a default to the model-neutral rule and is no conflict.
   - *Sentence 2 against STND.CONC.3.* A lock-free atomic is a facility of the language memory model; `volatile std::sig_atomic_t` for a signal handler is the protection the language defines for that case, and the interrupt of one thread by its own handler needs no ordering between threads. It is a stated protection in the sense of STND.CONC.3. They agree.
   - *HW.11.* HW.11 (SHALL) has each execution path verify its protocol under the model of its hardware contract, or of its language or library for a protocol that uses only their synchronization. It binds by its own text, so dropping the citation from STND.CXX.23 releases nothing. The citation survives in STND.CONC.3. Owner: HW (HW.2 row 1), unchanged. Pair 147 remains by DSGN.15.
   - *Onset.* Old sentence 1 was a default from CSQ3 (DSGN.15 and HW.11 as defaults). STND.CONC.3 applies from CSQ3 (Notes of STND.CONC, class paragraph: the protections, orderings, and lock rules of STND.CONC.3 to .9, the third sentence of .3 included, apply from CSQ3); sentence 2 of STND.CXX.23 is from CSQ3. The verification of the invariants stays at CSQ2 with DSGN.15 and HW.11. No onset moves. The Notes of STND.CXX say the same (class paragraph: STND.CONC.3 and .6 from CSQ3, .25 from CSQ2).
   - Result: nothing moves to another owner or onset. Compatible.
2. **STND.JL.27 against STND.CONC.3 and STND.CONC.16.**
   - *CONC.16.* Sentence 2 of STND.CONC.16: code SHOULD NOT select the state of a concurrent task by the identity of the thread that runs it (DSGN.14). STND.JL.27 (now one sentence) is its form for `Threads.threadid()`. It is narrower (one function) and gives the same direction; both can be met. The revision text and the Notes say so, correctly. Onset CSQ3 for both (STND.CONC lifecycle rules CONC.12 to .17 from CSQ3; JL default from CSQ3).
   - *CONC.3.* Old sentence 2 required that each mutable value changed by one task while another reads or changes it be guarded by a lock or atomic operations. STND.CONC.3 now holds that matter (one stated protection, followed at each access, by a lock or atomics among the possible means), and the Notes of STND.JL give the Julia examples (`push!` on a `Dict` needs a lock; accumulation inside `Threads.@threads` is a race; per-task partial results combined after `fetch`). The change is a loosening from "lock or atomic" to "one stated protection", the same one as for C++; no conflict.
   - *Findings.* Two points, Observations 1 and 2 below. Neither makes the two provisions unable to be met together.

## Observations

1. **Should-fix, STND.JL Notes, class paragraph.** It still reads "STND.JL.27 asks for the protocol of DSGN.15 from CSQ3; the verification of its invariants, which STND.CLASS.10 starts at CSQ2, stays with DSGN.15." After the amendment STND.JL.27 asks for no protocol; the stated protection is STND.CONC.3. Fix: replace with a sentence that STND.JL.27 applies from CSQ3 as the Julia form of the second sentence of STND.CONC.16, and leave the protocol and its verification to STND.CONC.3 and DSGN.15. Today the sentence is not false in effect (the onsets are right) but it describes a rule the provision no longer holds.
2. **Optional, STND.JL.27 front matter.** It instantiates DSGN.15 and cites (DSGN.15, STND.CONC.16), but STND.CONC.16 rests on DSGN.14 (state with one owner and a stated lifetime), and the Notes of STND.JL call it the Julia form of that sentence. Fix: instantiate DSGN.14 (alone, or with DSGN.15 if the race is the reason) and cite it. No onset moves (both default from CSQ3), no pair is lost except that the pairs would then be on DSGN.14.
3. **Optional, STND.PY.17 and STND.JL.24.** They derive the stream from "the index of the part", and say nothing on how the part is defined; STND.CONC.23 asks for a part defined independently of the number of tasks and of the task that computes it. A reader who takes one thread as one part meets STND.PY.17 and not STND.CONC.23. The Notes of STND.PY say STND.PY.17 is the Python form of STND.CONC.23; STND.JL.24 the same. No conflict (both are defaults and STND.CONC.23 applies on its own). A cite of STND.CONC.23 in each provision would close it; not needed.
4. **Optional, NUMER.2.** The matter is named for streams "of a computation divided among concurrent tasks", but the one provision given, NUMER.24, applies to every stochastic method that draws from more than one source. The keying and construction named in the Notes are held by LVL2 defaults, which a shared-matters table does not list. The Notes explain this correctly, so no ambiguity of ownership remains. If the custodian wants the table to show it, name the matter "Independence of random streams" instead. Preference only.
5. **Noted, not in scope.** STND.VVUQ.63 names a test battery in a provision, which the standing direction on tool names would put in a Note. It was adopted earlier and no amendment of this round changes it.
6. Pair 2341 of NUMER-pairs.md equals pair 81 of the CXX list. A reviewer of NUMER who covers 2341 there should note it is already covered here.

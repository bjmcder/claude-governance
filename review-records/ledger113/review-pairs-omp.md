## Method

- Read STND.HARN.28, `_doc.md` (Purpose, Terms, Notes) and the 27 provisions, and the bodies of the 79 distinct other provisions in the list (AGENT, CPS, HDES with its ASIC and FPGA sub-profiles, HW, HW.ASIC, HW.EMBED, HW.FPGA, HW.QPU, ML, NUMER), plus HW.3-HW.20, HW.CPU.3-5, HW.GPU.3-4, STND.CONC.1-29, STND.MPI.2, .3, .15, .18, .23, STND.CXX.22, .23, .26, STND.VVUQ.1, .2, .62, .82, .83, STND.ANNEX.6, AGENT.40, DSGN.1, DSGN.14, DSGN.15 and the Notes of HW, HW.CPU, HW.GPU, STND.CXX and STND.CONC where they speak of OpenMP, threads or offload.
- Completeness of the list: I recomputed the pair set from the front matter (each OMP provision against each provision of AGENT, CPS, HDES*, HW*, ML, MEAS, NUMER that instantiates one of the same DSGN, SIMPL, REPRO, VV, RSRC, INTPR, PRIME, PRIN, or QA provisions). It gives the same 336 pairs; none missing, none extra. No OMP provision has a pair on the "shared-matters table" basis: no table lists an OMP provision.
- Shared-matters tables read: HW.2, HW.CPU.2, HW.GPU.2, NUMER.2 and also CPS.2, ML.2, MEAS.2, HDES.2, HDES.ASIC.2, HDES.FPGA.2, HW.EMBED.2, HW.ASIC.2, HW.FPGA.2, HW.QPU.2; none of those rows concerns threads, tasks, synchronization, reductions, offload or runtime settings. Matters that the tables give to HW and that OMP touches: hardware contract of a path (HW.4-12, HW.CPU.3, HW.GPU.3), boundary contract (HW.13, HW.14), agreement between alternative paths (HW.15-19), budgets (HW.20, HW.21). NUMER gives tolerance basis (NUMER.11) and precision (NUMER.5, NUMER.12). HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.CPU.5 and HW.GPU.4 appear in no table row; see Observations.
- Grouping: pairs on unrelated subjects that share only a broad basis (VV.1, DSGN.5, DSGN.1, DSGN.15, DSGN.4, REPRO.7) are in group rows with one reason and the pair numbers. Every pair on the same subject (data sharing and state, synchronization and memory models, atomics, tasks and waits, reductions and determinism, runtime settings and records, offload placement and fallback, host-device data, device arithmetic, tests and checks) has its own row. The table lists each pair exactly once (checked by script: 336 pairs, numbers 1-336, no gap or repeat; 96 rows).
- Tests applied. Conflict: can one be met only by failing the other, or do they answer one question differently as written. Unowned: do they address one matter that no shared-matters table gives, where a profile would have to own it. A matter whose only profile provisions are in one profile, and where the other side is an LVL2 standard, is not counted as unowned unless the standard adds a matter that the profile does not hold (the precedent of the random-stream candidate, ledger 111 #79).
- Every OMP provision is a default (SHOULD). Where a profile provision binds (SHALL), a conflict is only recorded if the default cannot be met together with the profile provision; a default that adds to a profile provision is not a conflict.

## Observations (not conflicts, not unowned)

1. OMP.14 sentence 2 and the declared-variation rule: see the VVUQ.82 row. This is the only finding.
2. OMP.11 sentence 1: 'rely on a default memory order that a requires directive of its compilation unit declares' should say 'that orders them', or a declared relaxed default meets the sentence literally.
3. OMP.3 records the value at the start of the run of each control variable; a later change (a library calling a set-routine, which OMP.17 asks it not to do) is not in the record. HW.3 binds beyond it. Suggest 'and each change of it'.
4. OMP.19 sentence 1 is silent on the cancel construct; CONC.12 and CONC.13 have cancellation propagate. Suggest naming it as an allowed exit.
5. OMP.19 sentence 2 restates CXX.22 for structured blocks (GOV.31); the Purpose says those C++ rules are in STND.CXX. Either cite CXX.22 or keep it for the non-C++ reader.
6. The draft gives no route for the failure of a task or of a target region to reach its parent or to be observed (CONC.1, CONC.2, HW.GPU.3); those bind at their own strength. Acceptable under the additive design, but an adopter should know the gap is deliberate.
7. HW.3, HW.CPU.4, HW.CPU.5 and HW.GPU.4 are in no row of HW.2, HW.CPU.2 or HW.GPU.2. OMP.3 and OMP.17-18 touch the matters of the first three. HW is the only profile that addresses them, and OMP is an LVL2 standard, so there is no second profile to contest ownership and I did not count them as unowned. If the tables are meant to list every provision that two documents address, HW would own them.
